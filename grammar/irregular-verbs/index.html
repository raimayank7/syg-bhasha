Patterns that help

Same in all three forms (base, past, past participle) (13)

bet, cost, cut, hit, hurt, let, put, quit, read, set, shut, split, spread

Past and participle are the same, ending in -ought or -aught (6)

bring, buy, catch, fight, teach, think

Participle ends in -n, -en or -ne (and differs from the base) (34)

beat, begin, bite, blow, break, choose, do, draw, drive, eat, fall, fly, forget, forgive, freeze, get, give, go, grow, hide, know, ride, rise, see, shake, show, speak, steal, take, tear, throw, wake, wear, write

Participle is the same as the base verb (come, run) (3)

become, come, run

Past and participle are the same word (bend, bent, bent) (38)

bend, bind, bleed, breed, build, burn, deal, dig, feed, feel, find, have, hear, hold, keep, lay, lead, leave, lend, lose, make, mean, meet, pay, say, sell, send, shoot, sit, sleep, spend, spin, stand, stick, swing, tell, understand, win

Three different forms, with a vowel change (sing, sang, sung) (5)

drink, ring, sing, sink, swim

About this list

Every form was computed by the lemminflect package and then checked against the English Wiktionary: a verb is here only when its entry shows all the forms. A few verbs with two standard forms or meanings (such as lie, hang and light) are left out on purpose, and "be" (am, is, are, was, were, been) is covered by the conjugator. Some verbs have a second form that is used in British English (learnt, burnt, dreamt, and got for gotten); the table shows the American form. See present perfect and simple past for how the forms are used.
