Simple present tense

Form: base verb (add -s or -es for he, she, it).

How to form it

The simple present is made with base verb (add -s or -es for he, she, it). The questions and negatives use the same helping verbs. For example: "She works." and "They go."

Positive, negative and question: work

Iyouhe / she / itthey
PositiveI workyou workhe worksthey work
NegativeI do not workyou do not workhe does not workthey do not work
QuestionDo I work?Do you work?Does he work?Do they work?

The same with an irregular verb: go

Iyouhe / she / itthey
PositiveI goyou gohe goesthey go
NegativeI do not goyou do not gohe does not gothey do not go
QuestionDo I go?Do you go?Does he go?Do they go?

When to use it

  • Habits and routines: "She walks to school every day."
  • Facts that are always true: "Water boils at 100 degrees Celsius."
  • Timetables and schedules: "The train leaves at six."
  • States such as like, know, want and need: "I need help."

Words that often go with it

always, usually, often, sometimes, never, every day, on Mondays.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting the -s for he, she and it: write "he works", not "he work".
  • Using the continuous form for a habit: write "I walk to work", not "I am walking to work" (unless it is a temporary change).

Questions

How do I form the simple present?

Use base verb (add -s or -es for he, she, it).

Which time words go with the simple present?

Common ones are always, usually, often, sometimes, never, every day, on Mondays.
