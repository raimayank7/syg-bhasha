A, an and the: how to use articles in English

English has two kinds of article: the indefinite articles a and an, and the definite article the. Many languages, including Hindi and Punjabi, have no articles, so this is a common problem for learners. The rules below cover most cases.

A or an: listen to the sound

Use a before a consonant sound and an before a vowel sound. The rule is about the sound, not the spelling: an hour (the h is silent), a university (it begins with the sound "yoo"), an MBA (it begins with "em"), a one-rupee coin (it begins with "w").

A and an: one thing, not known or not yet mentioned

  • "I saw a dog in the street." (any dog, first time)
  • "She is an engineer." (a job or a type)
  • "It costs ten rupees a kilo." (per)

Use a and an only with singular nouns you can count. Do not use them with plural nouns or with uncountable nouns such as water, advice or information: write "some advice", not "an advice".

The: a particular thing that both people know

  • Already mentioned: "I saw a dog. The dog was black."
  • Only one exists: the sun, the moon, the Prime Minister, the Internet.
  • Made clear by the sentence: "the book on the table", "the girl who called you".
  • Superlatives and ordinals: the best, the first, the last.
  • Some names: the Ganga, the Himalayas, the United States, the Taj Mahal.

No article

  • General plurals and uncountable nouns: "Dogs are loyal." "Water is life."
  • Most names of people, cities and countries: "Delhi", "India", "Rohan".
  • Languages and subjects: "She speaks Hindi." "I study mathematics."
  • Meals, and some places in a fixed use: "have lunch", "go to school", "go to bed".

Common mistakes

  • "I have a information." Write "I have some information."
  • "He is engineer." Write "He is an engineer."
  • "I went to the school" when you mean as a student. Write "I went to school."
  • "The life is beautiful." Write "Life is beautiful."

Questions

Do I say a hour or an hour?

An hour, because the h is silent and the word begins with a vowel sound.

Do I use the with the names of countries?

Mostly not (India, France), but yes for names with a plural or a union word, such as the United States or the Netherlands.
