In, on and at: prepositions of time and place

The small words in, on and at cause many mistakes. Here is a rule of thumb for each, with the usual exceptions.

Time

UseForExamples
atexact times and short points in timeat 6 o'clock, at noon, at night, at the weekend (British), at Diwali
ondays and dateson Monday, on 15 August, on my birthday, on Monday morning
inlonger periods: months, years, seasons, parts of a dayin July, in 2026, in winter, in the morning, in the 21st century

Note: we say in the morning, in the afternoon and in the evening, but at night. When a day is named, use on: on Friday evening.

Place

UseForExamples
ata point or a place seen as a pointat the bus stop, at the door, at home, at school, at the airport
ona surface or a lineon the table, on the wall, on the second floor, on the road
inan enclosed space, or a larger areain the room, in the box, in Delhi, in India, in the car

Useful exceptions

  • We say in the car and in a taxi, but on the bus, on the train and on a plane.
  • We say at home but in the house.
  • We say at the hospital for a visit and in hospital (British) or in the hospital (American) for a patient.

Common mistakes

  • "I will meet you in Monday." Write "on Monday".
  • "She was born at 1998." Write "in 1998".
  • "He lives on Delhi." Write "in Delhi".
  • "I arrived to Mumbai." Write "I arrived in Mumbai" (or "at the station").

There are more uses of these words in phrases and in phrasal verbs, and those are best learned as whole phrases.

Questions

Do I say in the weekend or at the weekend?

British English says "at the weekend"; American English says "on the weekend".

Do I say on Sunday morning or in Sunday morning?

On Sunday morning. When a day is named, use on.
