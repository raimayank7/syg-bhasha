Language or dialect? Why the line is hard to draw

People often ask how many languages the world has, and whether a speech form is a language or a dialect. Both questions are harder than they look.

The usual test fails

A common rule says that two forms are dialects of one language if their speakers can understand each other. But understanding comes in degrees, it can be one-way, and it depends on exposure. Speakers of Spanish and Portuguese can follow each other quite well, yet they are different languages. Speakers of the different Chinese "dialects" often cannot understand each other, yet they are called dialects of Chinese.

Other things decide the label

  • Writing and a standard form. A form with a written standard and schools is usually called a language.
  • A state. A form with a country behind it tends to be called a language, which gave rise to the saying that a language is a dialect with an army and a navy.
  • Identity. People may feel that their speech belongs to a language community of its own, whatever linguists say.

What this means for counts

Different catalogues give different totals because they draw the line in different places. We do not give a total for the world and we do not claim that the languages on this site are all there are. Our list is the set that Unicode CLDR has data for.

How this site handles it

We follow the CLDR list and its names without taking a position. A speech form missing from our list is not a statement that it is "only" a dialect, and a form that appears is not a verdict that it is a separate language. If you think something is wrong, tell us.

Respect

Calling someone's language a "mere dialect" can sound like an insult. Where you can, use the name that its speakers use.

Questions

How many languages are there?

It depends on where you draw the line between language and dialect, so catalogues disagree. We give no total.

Can one language have two scripts?

Yes. Punjabi is written in Gurmukhi in India and in Shahmukhi in Pakistan, and Serbian in both Cyrillic and Latin.
