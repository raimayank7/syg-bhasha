What you can find here

Each language page gives the language's name in English and in itself, its usual writing system and direction, the countries where CLDR lists it and the estimated share of people who use it, the days of the week and the months in the language, and how a date and a number are written. Pages for scripts and countries link them together.

All the data is from Unicode CLDR; how to read the data explains the percentages, the scripts and the limits.

Questions

Where does the data come from?

From Unicode CLDR, the open locale database used in phones and computers, as shipped in the Babel package.

Why is my language missing?

CLDR lists the languages that have locale data, so many small languages are missing. We follow its list.
