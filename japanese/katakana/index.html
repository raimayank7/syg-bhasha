What Katakana is used for

Katakana is used for foreign words and names (such as the names of countries and foods from other languages), sound words, and sometimes for emphasis.

How to read the chart

Rows are read left to right. Each box shows the letter and below it the romaji. The row with ya, yu, yo has no "yi" or "ye" and the "wa" row has only wa and wo (written o in most words). The letter n stands alone. The marks " (dakuten) and ° (handakuten) change k into g, s into z, t into d, h into b and p. A combined sound is a letter of the i column followed by a small ya, yu or yo, as in kya.

Things to watch

  • shi, chi, tsu, fu and ji do not follow the pattern of their row, so the chart uses the Hepburn spelling, the one most learners see. Other systems write them si, ti, tu, hu and zi.
  • The letters ぢ (ji) and づ (zu) sound like じ and ず and are used only in a few words.
  • A small tsu doubles the next consonant and is not in the chart. A dash-like bar in katakana lengthens the vowel.
