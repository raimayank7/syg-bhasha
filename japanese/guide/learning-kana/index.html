How to learn hiragana and katakana: a two-week plan

People learn kana at different speeds. This plan is a guide, not a promise: fifteen to twenty minutes a day is enough for most learners to read hiragana in about a week and katakana in a second week, but go at your own pace.

Week 1: hiragana

  1. Day 1: a, i, u, e, o and ka, ki, ku, ke, ko. Say each letter aloud as you write it.
  2. Day 2: sa, shi, su, se, so and ta, chi, tsu, te, to. Note that shi, chi and tsu do not follow the pattern.
  3. Day 3: na, ni, nu, ne, no and ha, hi, fu, he, ho.
  4. Day 4: ma, mi, mu, me, mo and ya, yu, yo.
  5. Day 5: ra, ri, ru, re, ro and wa, wo, n.
  6. Day 6: the marked letters (ga, za, da, ba, pa) and the combined sounds (kya, sha and so on).
  7. Day 7: rest, then take the quiz on all hiragana.

Week 2: katakana

Use the same order. Katakana shapes are simpler and you already know the sounds, so most learners go faster. Do a few rows a day and finish with the quiz on both scripts.

Look-alikes to watch

  • Hiragana: さ and き, ぬ and め, わ and ね.
  • Katakana: シ and ツ, ソ and ン. The direction of the strokes tells them apart: write them by hand and look slowly.

Habits that work

  • Write letters by hand, slowly, while you say them.
  • Practise a little every day rather than a lot once a week.
  • Test yourself with the quiz and repeat only the letters you missed.
  • Read real words as early as you can: signs, menus, names of foods.

When to stop drilling

When you can read a row without pausing, move on. Drill speed comes from reading real text, not from ever more drills.

Questions

How long does it take to learn kana?

Many learners manage hiragana in about a week and katakana in another week with short daily practice, but it varies.

Should I learn katakana at the same time?

Most learners find it easier to finish hiragana first, then add katakana.
