How Japanese is written: hiragana, katakana and kanji together

A Japanese sentence normally contains three kinds of writing. That sounds hard, but each has a clear job, and a beginner can start with the two sound scripts in a few weeks.

The three kinds

  • Hiragana (46 basic signs) writes the sounds of the language. It is used for grammar words, for the endings of verbs and adjectives, and for words that have no kanji or whose kanji are hard. Children learn it first.
  • Katakana (the same 46 sounds, with squarer shapes) is used mainly for words from other languages, for foreign names, and for emphasis, a little as italics are used in English.
  • Kanji are characters that came from China. Each stands for a meaning (and usually has several readings). Nouns, the stems of verbs and adjectives, and many names are written with kanji.

In one sentence

In a typical sentence, the kanji carry the main meaning, the hiragana join the words and show grammar, and katakana mark foreign words. Reading it, you look at the shapes: dense, complicated characters are usually kanji, rounded ones are hiragana and angular ones are katakana.

How many signs

The two kana scripts have 46 basic signs each, plus signs with marks and combined sounds, which are the same signs put together (the charts here show 104 of each). Japan lists 2,136 kanji for general use (jōyō kanji): 1,026 are taught in elementary school and 1,110 afterwards. This site has a page for each of the first 1,026.

Romaji

Romaji means Japanese written in Latin letters. It is useful for a beginner, for typing and for signs for visitors, but real Japanese texts use kana and kanji, and the sooner you can read kana the better.

Direction

Modern Japanese is mostly written left to right in rows, as in English. Books, newspapers and manga often run in vertical columns from right to left.

Questions

Which should I learn first?

Hiragana, then katakana, then kanji a few at a time. Most learners can read both kana in a few weeks of short daily practice.

Do Japanese people use romaji?

Mainly for typing on a keyboard, for foreign visitors and for some names and brands. Ordinary texts use kana and kanji.
