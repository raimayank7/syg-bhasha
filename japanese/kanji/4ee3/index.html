Kanji 代

substitute, change, convert

代

代 is a kanji taught in grade 3 of Japanese elementary school. It is written with 5 strokes. In KANJIDIC2 its English meaning is substitute, change, convert, replace, period.

Its classical radical is number 9 (⼈, "Man"), the part that dictionaries use to look it up. Other grade 3 kanji with the same radical: 係, 仕, 使, 住, 他, 倍.

It is number 66 in a ranking of the 2,500 kanji most used in newspapers. KANJIDIC2 puts it at level 3 of the old four-level JLPT scale (1 is the hardest). Both are statistics from the file; see reading kanji for how to use them.

Among grade 3 kanji

Grade 3 has 200 kanji; 17 of them (including this one) have 5 strokes, which is fewer than the middle of the grade. Others with 5 strokes: 央, 去, 号, 皿, 仕, 写, 主, 申, 世, 他, 打, 皮.

Readings

On readings (from Chinese) are in katakana; kun readings (Japanese) are in hiragana, with a dot before the part that is written after the kanji (okurigana). The small letters are romaji.

How to write its readings

KANJIDIC2 lists 2 on readings and 9 kun readings for 代. On readings come from old Chinese and are mostly heard when the kanji sits inside a word of two or more kanji. Kun readings are native Japanese words, heard when the kanji stands almost alone, often followed by kana. Which reading a given word uses cannot be guessed from the kanji alone; you learn it together with the word.

This is how the first kun readings turn into writing:

  • か.わる (kawaru): the kanji carries the part before the dot, か, and the okurigana わる is written after it: 代わる.
  • かわ.る (kawaru): the kanji carries the part before the dot, かわ, and the okurigana る is written after it: 代る.
  • かわ.り (kawari): the kanji carries the part before the dot, かわ, and the okurigana り is written after it: 代り.

Kanji with the same sound

Japanese has many kanji that share a reading. Among the 1,026 elementary kanji, 4 others begin with the on reading dai (ダイ): 台 (pedestal); 大 (large); 第 (No.); 題 (topic). Heard without the written form, only the context tells you which one is meant.

Its radical family

Radical 9 is shared by 102 jōyō kanji in the file: 2 in grade 1, 5 in grade 2, 7 in grade 3, 18 in grade 4, 14 in grade 5, 7 in grade 6, 49 in secondary school. Kanji with one radical often have something in common in meaning, so learning them together can help memory, but the link is loose and there are many exceptions.

How hard is it to write?

With 5 strokes, 代 has more strokes than 83 of the 1,026 elementary kanji and fewer than 871. Stroke count is only one side of difficulty: shape, look-alike kanji and the number of readings matter too. This site does not show stroke order, so check it in a school book or a good dictionary before you practise.
