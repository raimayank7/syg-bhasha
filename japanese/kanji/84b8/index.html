Kanji 蒸

steam, heat, sultry

蒸

蒸 is a kanji taught in grade 6 of Japanese elementary school. It is written with 13 strokes. In KANJIDIC2 its English meaning is steam, heat, sultry, foment, get musty.

Its classical radical is number 140 (⾋, "Grass"), the part that dictionaries use to look it up. Other grade 6 kanji with the same radical: 若, 蔵, 著.

It is number 1552 in a ranking of the 2,500 kanji most used in newspapers. KANJIDIC2 puts it at level 2 of the old four-level JLPT scale (1 is the hardest). Both are statistics from the file; see reading kanji for how to use them.

Among grade 6 kanji

Grade 6 has 191 kanji; 15 of them (including this one) have 13 strokes, which is more than the middle of the grade. Others with 13 strokes: 絹, 源, 署, 傷, 聖, 誠, 暖, 腸, 賃, 腹, 幕, 盟.

Readings

On readings (from Chinese) are in katakana; kun readings (Japanese) are in hiragana, with a dot before the part that is written after the kanji (okurigana). The small letters are romaji.

How to write its readings

KANJIDIC2 lists 2 on readings and 3 kun readings for 蒸. On readings come from old Chinese and are mostly heard when the kanji sits inside a word of two or more kanji. Kun readings are native Japanese words, heard when the kanji stands almost alone, often followed by kana. Which reading a given word uses cannot be guessed from the kanji alone; you learn it together with the word.

This is how the first kun readings turn into writing:

  • む.す (musu): the kanji carries the part before the dot, む, and the okurigana す is written after it: 蒸す.
  • む.れる (mureru): the kanji carries the part before the dot, む, and the okurigana れる is written after it: 蒸れる.
  • む.らす (murasu): the kanji carries the part before the dot, む, and the okurigana らす is written after it: 蒸らす.

Kanji with the same sound

Japanese has many kanji that share a reading. Among the 1,026 elementary kanji, 9 others begin with the on reading jou (ジョウ): 上 (above); 乗 (ride); 城 (castle); 場 (location); 常 (usual); 情 (feelings); 条 (article); 状 (status quo); 縄 (straw rope). Heard without the written form, only the context tells you which one is meant.

Its radical family

Radical 140 is shared by 46 jōyō kanji in the file: 2 in grade 1, 1 in grade 2, 5 in grade 3, 5 in grade 4, 4 in grade 6, 29 in secondary school. Kanji with one radical often have something in common in meaning, so learning them together can help memory, but the link is loose and there are many exceptions.

How hard is it to write?

With 13 strokes, 蒸 has more strokes than 833 of the 1,026 elementary kanji and fewer than 135. Stroke count is only one side of difficulty: shape, look-alike kanji and the number of readings matter too. This site does not show stroke order, so check it in a school book or a good dictionary before you practise.
