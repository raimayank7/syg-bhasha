Kanji 足

leg, foot, be sufficient

足

足 is a kanji taught in grade 1 of Japanese elementary school. It is written with 7 strokes. In KANJIDIC2 its English meaning is leg, foot, be sufficient, counter for pairs of footwear.

Its classical radical is number 157 (⾜, "Foot"), the part that dictionaries use to look it up.

It is number 343 in a ranking of the 2,500 kanji most used in newspapers. KANJIDIC2 puts it at level 4 of the old four-level JLPT scale (1 is the hardest). Both are statistics from the file; see reading kanji for how to use them.

Among grade 1 kanji

Grade 1 has 80 kanji; 9 of them (including this one) have 7 strokes, which is more than the middle of the grade. Others with 7 strokes: 花, 貝, 見, 車, 赤, 村, 男, 町.

Readings

On readings (from Chinese) are in katakana; kun readings (Japanese) are in hiragana, with a dot before the part that is written after the kanji (okurigana). The small letters are romaji.
