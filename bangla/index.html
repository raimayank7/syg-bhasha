    Who this site is for

    It is for the person posted to Kolkata or Siliguri for work, the student joining a college in Bengal, the family with new Bengali relatives, the neighbour who wants to say more than "nomoshkar", and the lover of Bengali songs, films and sweets who would like to read a signboard. If you speak and read Hindi, you can begin reading Bangla in days.

    Why Hindi makes Bangla easy

    The two are sister languages. The Bengali script is built exactly like Devanagari, in the same letter order, with the same kind of vowel signs and conjuncts. Sentences run in the same order, with the verb at the end. Thousands of words are shared, with a regular change of sound: सुंदर becomes shundor, विजय becomes bijoy, नमस्कार becomes nomoshkar. And Bangla has no grammatical gender at all, so there is no करता / करती to worry about.

    Common questions

    Is Bangla easy for Hindi speakers?

    Yes, comparatively. The script is built like Devanagari, the sentence order is the same, many words are shared, and Bangla has no grammatical gender. The verbs and their endings are different and need practice, and so does the pronunciation.

    Is it Bangla or Bengali?

    Both are right. "Bangla" is the name of the language in the language itself. "Bengali" is the usual English name for the language and its people.

    Why is "namaskar" said "nomoshkar" in Bangla?

    Two regular sound rules are at work: the short "a" built into each consonant is said as a rounded "o", and the letter s is said "sh". The spelling is the same as in Sanskrit; only the pronunciation differs.

    Is water "jol" or "pani" in Bangla?

    Both. "Jol" is more usual in West Bengal and "pani" in Bangladesh, and each is understood everywhere. The page on West Bengal and Bangladesh words lists more such pairs.
