A cousin of Devanagari

The Bengali script is used to write Bangla and, with small differences, Assamese; it is also used for some other languages of eastern India, among them Manipuri alongside its own script. Like Devanagari it descends from the Brahmi script of ancient India, and the two are built on exactly the same plan. Each consonant carries a built-in vowel. Other vowels are shown by signs attached to the consonant. Consonants that meet without a vowel are joined into conjuncts. The letters are even recited in the same order.

For a reader of Hindi, then, learning this script is a matter of matching new shapes to letters already known. This site places the Devanagari twin beside every Bengali letter and sign to make that matching easy.

What is different

  • The built-in vowel is "o", not "a".
  • Several vowel signs stand to the left of their consonant, and two wrap around it.
  • Pronunciation has moved away from spelling in regular ways: sh for s, b for v, j for y, and silent letters inside conjuncts.
  • The digits are different and are in everyday use; see numbers.

How to use these lessons

Take them in order: vowels, consonants, vowel signs, conjuncts. Write as you read, with the Hindi letter beside the Bangla one. Then read the cousin words, which you can check against your Hindi, and test yourself on the practice page. The ten day plan sets out a daily route, and Bangla pronunciation explains why the words do not sound the way they are spelt.
