Bangla vowel signs (kar)

As in Hindi, a vowel that follows a consonant is not written with its full letter. It is written with a short sign attached to the consonant. Hindi calls these signs मात्रा. Bangla calls each one a কার (kar): আ-কার (a-kar) is the sign of আ, ই-কার (i-kar) the sign of ই, and so on.

The system is the one you know, sign for sign. The inherent vowel "o" has no sign. The i-kar is written before the consonant although it is spoken after it, exactly like the Hindi ि. The differences are of shape and position, and three signs deserve attention:

  • The e-kar ে is also written before the consonant, on the left. In Hindi the matra of ए sits on top.
  • The oi-kar ৈ is likewise on the left.
  • The o-kar ো and ou-kar ৌ wrap around the consonant, with one part on the left and one on the right.

So when you see a hook to the left of a letter, look to the right as well before you decide which vowel it is.

Read these aloud

Try the signs on words you can guess from Hindi: নাম (nam, name), দিন (din, day), নদী (nodi, river), গুরু (guru), দূর (dur, far), দেশ (desh, country), মেঘ (megh, cloud), লোক (lok, people), বৈশাখ (Boishakh), মৌমাছি (moumachhi, honeybee). In দেশ the hook stands to the left of দ, yet you read d first and e second.

Signs with special shapes

A few consonants change their look when certain signs are added, and these have to be recognised as wholes: গু (gu), শু (shu), রু (ru), রূ (ruu), হু (hu), হৃ (hri). Hindi has the same habit in रु and रू. Modern fonts often use plainer, more regular forms, so you may see either.

The e that sounds like "a" in "cat"

The vowel এ and its sign are sometimes said like the "a" in the English "cat". The commonest example is এক (one), which many speakers say "ak"; another is দেখা (to see), "dakha". The same sound is written with jo-phola plus a-kar in words such as ব্যাগ (bag) and ব্যাংক (bank). There is no rule a beginner can apply; the roman readings on this site use plain "e", and your ear will pick up the difference from speakers.
