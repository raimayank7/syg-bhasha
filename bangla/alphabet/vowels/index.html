Bangla vowels

The Bengali alphabet is called বর্ণমালা (bornomala), "the garland of letters", the same word as the Hindi वर्णमाला. It begins, as Devanagari does, with the vowels, called স্বরবর্ণ (shoroborno). There are eleven in use today.

If you read Hindi, you start with a great advantage. The Bengali script and Devanagari are cousins, both descended from the ancient Brahmi script, and they are arranged in exactly the same order: a, aa, i, ee, u, oo and so on. Every Bengali vowel has a Devanagari twin. You are not learning a new system, only new shapes for a system you know.

The twist is in the sounds. Bangla spelling follows Sanskrit, but Bangla pronunciation has moved on. The very first letter, অ, is the twin of अ but is said as a short rounded "o", like the o in the English "hot". And the long and short pairs (ই ঈ, উ ঊ) are written differently but pronounced the same length in ordinary speech.

The inherent vowel: the key to Bangla sound

In Hindi, a consonant written alone is read with a short अ: क is "ka". In Bangla, a consonant written alone is read with অ: ক is "ko". This single difference is what makes Bangla sound so round to a Hindi ear. कमल (kamal) is কমল, said "komol". रवींद्र is "Robindro".

The inherent vowel has two shades. Usually it is the open "o" of "hot". Before a syllable with i or u, and in some other positions, it closes to the "o" of "go": অতি is "oti" with a closed o. Do not worry about choosing; if you say a short "o" every time you will be understood, and your ear will sort out the rest.

At the end of many words the inherent vowel is dropped, as in Hindi: নাম is "nam", not "namo". But after a conjunct it is usually kept: গল্প is "golpo" (story).

Three things Hindi speakers get wrong

  • Saying অ as "a". অনেক is "onek", not "anek".
  • Saying ঐ and ঔ as in Hindi. The Hindi ऐ is like the vowel of "cat" and औ like "saw". The Bangla letters are true double vowels: "oi" and "ou". বৈশাখ is "Boishakh".
  • Stretching the long vowels. ঈ and ঊ matter for spelling, not for length.

Each vowel also has a short sign that attaches to a consonant, just like the Hindi matras. Those are on the vowel signs page.
