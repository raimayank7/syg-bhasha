Bangla conjuncts (juktakkhor)

When two or more consonants come together with no vowel between them, Bangla joins them into a single shape called a যুক্তাক্ষর (juktakkhor), "joined letter". Hindi does the same, calling it संयुक्ताक्षर. क् + त = क्त; in Bangla, ক্ + ত = ক্ত.

Bangla has a large number of conjuncts, and this is the part of the script that takes longest to master. There are three kinds:

  1. Transparent ones, where both letters can still be seen, side by side or one above the other: ল্ল (l + l), ন্ন (n + n), স্ক (s + k).
  2. Ones with a fixed attachment, where the second letter takes a special small form: the phola signs for য, র, ব, ল, ম, ন, and the reph for a preceding র.
  3. Opaque ones, whose shape gives little hint of the parts: ক্ষ (k + sh), জ্ঞ (j + n), ঞ্চ (n + ch), ঙ্গ (ng + g), হ্ম (h + m). These must be learnt as new letters.

Why the sound differs from the spelling

Three habits explain most of the surprises in the "said as" column.

  • A joined য or ব is not said; it doubles the consonant before it. সত্য is not "satya" but "shotto". বিশ্ব (world) is "bissho". পদ্ম (lotus) is "poddo", because a joined ম often behaves the same way. At the start of a word nothing is doubled: স্বামী is simply "shami".
  • ক্ষ is "kh". At the start of a word it is "kh" (ক্ষমা, khoma); in the middle it is "kkh" (লক্ষ্মী, lokkhi; শিক্ষা, shikkha).
  • জ্ঞ is "g". জ্ঞান (knowledge) is close to "gyan" with a nasal vowel, and বিজ্ঞান (science) is "biggan".

This is why Bengali names sound as they do. लक्ष्मी becomes Lokkhi, सत्यजित becomes Shottojit, and विश्वास becomes Bishshash.

The reph and the ro-phola

The letter র has two special joined forms, as in Hindi. When "r" comes before another consonant it is written as a small stroke on top of that consonant, the reph: কর্ম (kormo), সূর্য (shurjo, sun), like the Hindi कर्म, सूर्य. When "r" comes after a consonant it is a wavy line below, the ro-phola: প্রথম (prothom), গ্রাম (gram, village).

How to learn conjuncts

  1. Do not try to learn them from a chart. There are far too many. Learn each one inside a word, when you meet it.
  2. Use Hindi. If you can guess the word from Hindi (আনন্দ, आनंद), the conjunct has just taught itself to you.
  3. Start with the twenty-four above. They cover a large share of everyday text.
  4. Read signboards and headlines, which are full of them, and keep a list of new ones in your notebook with one word for each.
