Bangla consonants

The consonants, ব্যঞ্জনবর্ণ (byanjonborno), follow the same scientific order as in Devanagari: five rows of five, arranged by where in the mouth the sound is made, from the throat forward to the lips, followed by the semi-vowels and the sibilants. If you can recite क ख ग घ ङ, you can already recite the Bengali alphabet; you only need to say "ko kho go gho ngo".

That is the first point: each letter is named with the inherent vowel "o", not "a". The second is that every Bengali consonant has an exact Devanagari twin in the same position, so the table below is really a list of new shapes for old friends. Some shapes are even alike: compare গ with ग, and ম with म.

Most Bengali letters hang from a horizontal line at the top, the মাত্রা (matra), like the Hindi शिरोरेखा. A few, such as এ, ঙ and ৎ, have none, and some have only half a line.

Five differences from Hindi

  1. য is "j". The twin of य is pronounced exactly like জ at the start of a word: যমুনা is "Jomuna", যদি (if) is "jodi". For the real "y" sound Bangla added a dot underneath: য়, as in সময় (shomoy).
  2. One letter for b and v. ব serves for both ब and व, and is always said "b". So विद्या is "biddya" and वसंत is "boshonto". The "w" sound in foreign words is written ওয়.
  3. Three letters, one "sh". শ, ষ and স are all normally said "sh". সাত (seven) is "shat". A true "s" is heard mainly when স is joined to t, th, n, r or l, as in স্থান (sthan, place) and আস্তে (aste, slowly).
  4. Two n's, one sound. ণ and ন are both a plain "n". The spelling follows Sanskrit.
  5. ফ leans towards "f". Many speakers say it with the lips barely closing, so ফুল (flower) may sound like "phul" or "ful".

Letters that look alike

Beginners confuse a few pairs. Put them side by side and find the difference: ব (b) and র (r, with a dot below); ড (d) and ড় (flapped r, dot below); য (j) and য় (y, dot below); খ (kh) and থ (th); ঘ (gh) and ষ (sh); ন (n) and ণ (n); ত (t) and অ (the vowel o).

How to learn them

Go row by row, saying the Hindi letter as you write the Bangla one: write ক, say "क, ko". Five letters a session is plenty. After each row, read the example words on the cousin words page that use those letters, and test yourself on the practice page.
