Introducing yourself

14 phrases in Bangla, each in the script with a roman reading, the Hindi meaning and the English meaning. Tap Listen to hear it, where your device has a Bangla voice.

Introductions show how close Bangla sentence-building is to Hindi. আমার নাম অমন (amar nam Aman) is word for word "मेरा नाम अमन". But look at what is missing: there is no "is". Bangla drops the verb "to be" in simple present sentences. "मैं छात्र हूँ" is just আমি ছাত্র (ami chhatro), "I student".

Three pronouns do the work of the Hindi three. আপনি (apni) is आप, তুমি (tumi) is तुम, and তুই (tui) is तू. Use apni with strangers, elders and at work. The possessive forms are আপনার (apnar, आपका), তোমার (tomar, तुम्हारा) and আমার (amar, मेरा).

Here is a relief for Hindi speakers: Bangla has no grammatical gender. A man and a woman both say ami kaj kori, "I work"; there is no करता / करती to choose. Adjectives do not change either.

  • আপনার নাম কী?

    apnar nam ki?

  • আমার বাড়ি পাঞ্জাবে

    amar bari panjabe

    मेरा घर पंजाब में है

    My home is in Punjab

How these sentences are built

  • থেকে (theke) is "from", like से. -এ / -য় / -তে (-e, -y, -te) added to a place is "in": পাঞ্জাবে in Punjab, লুধিয়ানায় in Ludhiana, দিল্লিতে in Delhi.
  • A yes-or-no question is made by putting কি (ki) after the subject: আপনি কি হিন্দি জানেন? The question word "what" is spelt differently, কী, with the long vowel sign.
  • The verb ending tells you who is acting: -i for I (করি), -o for tumi (করো), -en for apni (করেন). More in Bangla grammar for Hindi speakers.
