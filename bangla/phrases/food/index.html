Food and eating

15 phrases in Bangla, each in the script with a roman reading, the Hindi meaning and the English meaning. Tap Listen to hear it, where your device has a Bangla voice.

Bengal takes its food seriously, and a guest who can say a few words about it wins hearts at once. The saying goes মাছে ভাতে বাঙালি (machhe bhate bangali): fish and rice make a Bengali. ভাত (bhat) is cooked rice, মাছ (machh) is fish, and মিষ্টি (mishti) means both "sweet" and "sweets", of which Bengal is very proud.

One word on this page splits the Bangla-speaking world. Water is জল (jol) in West Bengal and পানি (pani) in Bangladesh and among many Muslim speakers in India. Both are understood everywhere. Similarly, salt is নুন (nun) or লবণ (lobon).

If you are vegetarian, the word to know is নিরামিষ (niramish). In Bengal it traditionally also means cooked without onion and garlic, so to be clear, say what you do not eat: আমি মাছ-মাংস খাই না, "I do not eat fish or meat". Note the verb খাওয়া (khaoa): in Bangla you "eat" not only food but also water, tea and even a cigarette. চা খাবেন? is "will you have tea?"

Words of the Bengali table

ভাত bhat (rice), ডাল dal, তরকারি torkari (vegetable curry), ভাজা bhaja (fried things), লুচি luchi (puri of white flour), মাছের ঝোল machher jhol (fish curry), চাটনি chatni, দই doi (curd), রসগোল্লা roshogolla, সন্দেশ shondesh. More on the food words page.

Tastes: মিষ্টি mishti (sweet), ঝাল jhal (hot, chilli-hot), টক tok (sour), নোনতা nonta (salty), তেতো teto (bitter).
