Festival and occasion greetings

14 phrases in Bangla, each in the script with a roman reading, the Hindi meaning and the English meaning. Tap Listen to hear it, where your device has a Bangla voice.

Most Bangla greetings for special days are made from one small word: শুভ (shubho), "auspicious, good", the Hindi शुभ. Put it before the occasion and the greeting is ready: শুভ জন্মদিন (shubho jonmodin), happy birthday; শুভ নববর্ষ (shubho noboborsho), happy new year.

The Bengali new year, পয়লা বৈশাখ (Poila Boishakh), the first day of the month of Boishakh, falls in mid-April. The biggest festival of West Bengal is Durga Puja in autumn, and its greetings change by the day: শারদীয় শুভেচ্ছা (sharodiyo shubhechchha), "autumn-festival good wishes", during the Puja, and শুভ বিজয়া (shubho bijoya) from Dashami, the last day, when the goddess departs. After Bijoya, the young touch the feet of elders and friends embrace; the embrace is called কোলাকুলি (kolakuli).

Greetings of other communities are just as much a part of Bangla: ঈদ মোবারক (Eid Mubarak) and শুভ বড়দিন (shubho borodin), "happy big day", for Christmas. All of these are ready to copy and send.

    bhalo theko

    Stay well (to someone younger or a friend)

  • আশীর্বাদ করুন

    ashirbad korun

    आशीर्वाद दीजिए

    Please give me your blessing

  • নতুন বছর ভালো কাটুক

    notun bochhor bhalo katuk

    नया साल अच्छा बीते

    May the new year go well

Notes

  • শুভেচ্ছা (shubhechchha) is "good wishes", from शुभ + इच्छा. অভিনন্দন (obhinondon) is "congratulations", for an achievement.
  • To elders, add প্রণাম নেবেন (pronam neben), "please accept my pranam", in a message. To those younger, elders write আশীর্বাদ নিও (ashirbad nio), "take my blessings".
  • The festival dates follow the Bengali and lunar calendars and change every year in the common calendar, so we do not list dates here.
