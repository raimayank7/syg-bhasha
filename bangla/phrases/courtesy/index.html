Yes, no, please, thank you

16 phrases in Bangla, each in the script with a roman reading, the Hindi meaning and the English meaning. Tap Listen to hear it, where your device has a Bangla voice.

The small words of politeness are the ones you will use from the first minute. Bangla keeps them simple. "Yes" is হ্যাঁ (hyan), a short nasal sound close to the Hindi हाँ. "No" is না (na). "All right" is ঠিক আছে (thik achhe), the twin of ठीक है.

"Thank you" is ধন্যবাদ (dhonnobad), the same word as the Hindi धन्यवाद in Bengali sound. As in much of India, people do not thank family and close friends in words for small things; a smile does it. With strangers, shopkeepers and in formal settings, ধন্যবাদ is right.

"Please" is দয়া করে (doya kore), literally "doing kindness", like कृपा करके. It is used less than "please" in English, because politeness is already built into the verb: বসুন (boshun) is "please sit" on its own. For "sorry" and "excuse me", the common phrase is মাফ করবেন (maf korben), "please forgive"; the more formal word is দুঃখিত (dukkhito).

Notes

  • না comes after the verb in Bangla, where Hindi puts नहीं before it: আমি জানি না, word for word "I know not".
  • একটু (ektu), "a little", softens any request, exactly like ज़रा.
  • The polite request form ends in -ুন (-un): বলুন, বসুন, আসুন, দেখুন, like the Hindi -इए of बोलिए, बैठिए.
  • Shaking the head from side to side with আচ্ছা (achchha) means "I see" or "okay", just as in Hindi.
