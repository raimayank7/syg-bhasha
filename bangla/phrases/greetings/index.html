Greetings

15 phrases in Bangla, each in the script with a roman reading, the Hindi meaning and the English meaning. Tap Listen to hear it, where your device has a Bangla voice.

Bangla greetings are short and warm, and the first of them will sound familiar to any Hindi speaker: নমস্কার. It is the same word as नमस्कार, but Bengalis say it "nomoshkar", with rounded o sounds and a soft "sh". That one word already teaches two of the main habits of Bangla pronunciation, which you will meet on every page of this site.

The standard way to ask after someone is কেমন আছেন? (kemon achhen?), "how are you?", and the standard answer is ভালো আছি (bhalo achhi), "I am well". The verb changes with respect, as in Hindi. To an elder or a stranger you say achhen (with आप); to a friend, achho (with तुम); to a child or a very close friend, achhish (with तू).

Bangla is spoken in West Bengal, Tripura and the Barak Valley of Assam in India, and it is the national language of Bangladesh. Greetings vary a little by community: নমস্কার is common among Hindus and as a general formal greeting in India, while Muslims in both countries commonly greet with আসসালামু আলাইকুম (assalamu alaikum).

  • নমস্কার

    nomoshkar

    नमस्कार

    Hello (respectful)

  • কেমন আছেন?

    kemon achhen?

    आप कैसे हैं?

    How are you? (polite)

  • কেমন আছো?

    kemon achho?

    तुम कैसे हो?

    How are you? (to a friend)

  • আমি ভালো আছি

    ami bhalo achhi

    मैं ठीक हूँ

    I am well

  • আপনি কেমন আছেন?

    apni kemon achhen?

    और आप कैसे हैं?

    And how are you?

  • অনেক দিন পর দেখা হলো

    onek din por dekha holo

    बहुत दिनों बाद मिले

    We meet after a long time

    apnar shathe dekha hoye bhalo laglo

    आपसे मिलकर अच्छा लगा

    It was good to meet you

    shubho shondhya

    शुभ संध्या

    Good evening

    shubho ratri

    शुभ रात्रि

    ashun, boshun

    We will meet again

  • আসি

    ashi

    चलता हूँ (शब्दशः: आता हूँ)

    Goodbye (literally "I come")

  • ভালো থাকবেন

    bhalo thakben

    अपना ध्यान रखिएगा, ठीक रहिएगा

    Stay well; take care

Notes

  • A Bengali who is leaving does not say "I am going". That is thought unlucky. He says আসি (ashi), "I come", meaning "I will come again". The host replies এসো (esho) or আসুন (ashun), "do come".
  • শুভ সকাল and শুভ রাত্রি are understood everywhere but sound a little bookish. In daily life people mostly say নমস্কার, or simply ask কেমন আছেন?
  • Elders are greeted by touching their feet (প্রণাম, pronam), especially at festivals such as Bijoya.
  • The spelling স্বাগতম has a "w" (ব-phola) that is not pronounced; it only strengthens the s. Many written letters in Bangla conjuncts are silent in this way. See Bangla pronunciation.
