Bangla numbers and numerals

23 words in Bangla, each in the script with a roman reading, the Hindi meaning and the English meaning. Tap Listen to hear it, where your device has a Bangla voice.

Bangla numbers are close relatives of the Hindi ones, since both come down from Sanskrit: ek, dui, tin, char, panch. A Hindi speaker can count to ten in Bangla after one reading. The differences are in pronunciation: सात becomes shat, दस becomes dosh, and छह becomes chhoy.

Bangla has its own digits, and unlike in Hindi, where international digits have largely replaced the Devanagari ones, the Bengali digits are in full everyday use: on price tags, bus numbers, calendars, banknotes and newspapers. They are worth learning at once.

Digit0123456789
Bangla০১২৩৪৫৬৭৮৯
Devanagari०१२३४५६७८९

Watch two traps. The Bangla ৪ (4) looks like the international 8, and the Bangla ৭ (7) looks like the international 9. Many a visitor has misread a price or a bus number because of them.

Notes

  • Twenty has two words: কুড়ি (kuri), usual in West Bengal, and বিশ (bish), usual in Bangladesh.
  • When counting things, Bangla adds -টা (-ta) to the number, or -জন (-jon) for people: দুটো বই (duto boi) two books, তিনজন লোক (tinjon lok) three people. "Two" changes from dui to du- when joined.
  • Money: টাকা (taka) is the everyday word for rupees in West Bengal and the name of Bangladesh's currency. দশ টাকা is ten rupees.
  • Time: কটা বাজে? (kota baje?) "What time is it?" পাঁচটা বাজে (panchta baje) "It is five o'clock." সাড়ে (share) is "half past", সোয়া (shoa) "quarter past", পৌনে (poune) "quarter to", just like साढ़े, सवा, पौने.
