Common Bangla verbs with "I" forms

16 words in Bangla, each in the script with a roman reading, the Hindi meaning and the English meaning. Tap Listen to hear it, where your device has a Bangla voice.

A Bangla verb changes its ending for the person, but, happily, not for gender and not for number. "I go" is আমি যাই (ami jai) whether a boy or a girl says it, and "we go" uses the very same verb: আমরা যাই (amra jai). Compare the Hindi जाता हूँ, जाती हूँ, जाते हैं, जाती हैं.

This list gives each verb in the simple present with আমি (ami, I), which always ends in -ই (-i). From that one form you can make the others by changing the ending:

Three more things you can do with a verb

  • Say no: put না after it: আমি খাই না (ami khai na), "I do not eat (it)".
  • Happening now: change -i to -chhi: আমি করছি (ami korchhi), "I am doing"; আমি যাচ্ছি (ami jachchhi), "I am going".
  • Polite request: the ending -un: করুন (korun) please do, বলুন (bolun) please say, দেখুন (dekhun) please look.

The vowel inside some verbs shifts between forms, for example শোনা (shona) but শুনি (shuni), and লেখা (lekha) but লিখি (likhi). Learn these as you meet them. The wider picture is in Bangla grammar for Hindi speakers.
