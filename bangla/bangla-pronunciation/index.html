Bangla

Bangla pronunciation for Hindi speakers

A Hindi speaker who learns the Bengali letters can read a Bangla sentence aloud on the first day, and a Bengali listener will smile, because it will sound like Sanskrit recited in Hindi. Bangla spelling is conservative; it mostly keeps the Sanskrit form of a word. Bangla speech has changed a good deal. This page gathers the regular differences, so that what you read comes out sounding like Bangla.

The seven main rules

1. The inherent vowel is "o", not "a"

Every consonant carries a short rounded "o". কমল is "komol", নমস্কার is "nomoshkar", রবি is "robi". This one change does more than any other to make your Bangla sound right.

2. s is "sh"

শ ষ স are all "sh" in most positions. সকাল (morning) is "shokal"; সব (all) is "shob"; মাস (month) is "mash". A clear "s" is kept when স joins t, th, n, r, l or k in a conjunct: রাস্তা rasta, স্নান snan, স্কুল skul; and in many English loanwords.

3. v is "b"

There is no v. বন is "bon" for वन; বিকাশ is "bikash" for विकास. That is why the same name is Vivek in Delhi and Bibek in Kolkata.

4. y is "j" at the start

য at the beginning of a word or syllable is "j": যোগ jog, যাত্রা jatra, যুবক jubok. The y sound lives on in য়, found in the middle and at the end of words: সময় shomoy, মেয়ে meye.

5. Joined y, v and often m are silent, and double the consonant

সত্য shotto, বিদ্যা biddya, বিশ্ব bissho, পদ্ম poddo, আত্মা atta (soul; with a nasal colour). At the start of a word they are simply dropped: স্বাধীন shadhin (free), জ্বর jor (fever), ত্যাগ tæg (giving up).

6. ksh is "kh" and jn is "g"

ক্ষ: ক্ষতি khoti (harm), পরীক্ষা porikkha (examination), লক্ষ lokkho (lakh). জ্ঞ: জ্ঞান gyan, অজ্ঞান oggan (unconscious).

7. Long and short vowels sound alike; the two n's sound alike

দিন (day) and দীন (poor) are said the same. So are ন and ণ. The spelling distinguishes them for the eye only.

Does a word end in "o" or not?

The inherent vowel at the end of a word is usually dropped, as in Hindi: নাম nam, ফল phol, হাত hat. It is kept (1) after a conjunct: গল্প golpo, শান্ত shanto, বন্ধ bondho; (2) in many adjectives and verb forms: ভালো bhalo, বড় boro (big), ছোট chhoto (small), করো koro; and (3) in some Sanskrit words. Modern spelling often writes this final o plainly with an o-kar, which helps.

The music of the language

Bangla stresses the first syllable of a word, gently, and lets the voice fall after it. Hindi stress is more even. Bangla also softens the breathy consonants in the middle and end of words, so that দুধ (milk) may sound close to "dud". Listening to songs, radio and films will teach you this better than any description.

A warning about roman spelling

There is no single agreed way of writing Bangla in English letters. Bengali names are often spelt the Sanskrit way (Satyajit, Vidyasagar, Lakshmi) though they are said the Bengali way. The roman readings on this site follow the sound: o for the inherent vowel, sh for the s-letters, j for য. They are a guide for your first weeks, not a standard.

Practise the rules on the cousin words, and see the letters behind them on the consonants and conjuncts pages.
