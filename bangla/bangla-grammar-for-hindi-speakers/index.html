Bangla

Bangla grammar for Hindi speakers

Bangla and Hindi are sister languages, and their grammar shows it: the verb comes last, small words follow the noun, and respect is built into the verb. But Bangla has simplified some things Hindi keeps, and kept a few that Hindi has lost. This page walks through the main points, always starting from the Hindi you know.

1. No grammatical gender

This is the best news. Bangla nouns have no gender. Adjectives do not change, and verbs do not change for gender.

HindiBangla
लड़का जाता है / लड़की जाती हैছেলেটি যায় / মেয়েটি যায় (chheleti jay / meyeti jay)
अच्छा लड़का / अच्छी लड़कीভালো ছেলে / ভালো মেয়ে (bhalo chhele / bhalo meye)

Even "he" and "she" are one word: সে (she), or respectfully তিনি (tini).

2. The verb changes for person and respect, not for number

"I go" and "we go" use the same verb form. What matters is who the subject is and how much respect is due.

SubjectBangla pronoun"go" (present)Hindi
I / weআমি / আমরা ami / amraযাই jaiजाता हूँ / जाते हैं
you (intimate)তুই tuiযাস jashतू जाता है
you (friendly)তুমি tumiযাও jaoतुम जाते हो
you (respectful)আপনি apniযান janआप जाते हैं
he / she (ordinary)সে sheযায় jayवह जाता है
he / she (respectful)তিনি tiniযান janवे जाते हैं

Notice that the respectful "you" and the respectful "he/she" share one verb form, as आप and वे do in Hindi.

3. "Is" is usually missing

In a simple statement that one thing is another, Bangla uses no verb: আমি ছাত্র (ami chhatro), "I [am] a student"; এটা বই (eta boi), "this [is] a book". To say "is not", use নই / নয় (noi / noy): আমি ডাক্তার নই, "I am not a doctor".

For "there is" and "have", the verb is আছে (achhe), and its negative is the single word নেই (nei): আমার সময় আছে (amar shomoy achhe), "I have time", built just like "मेरे पास समय है"; সময় নেই, "there is no time".

4. Case endings and postpositions

Where Hindi uses separate postpositions, Bangla mostly glues an ending to the noun.

HindiBanglaExample
का, की, के-র / -এর (-r / -er)রামের বই Ramer boi, Ram's book
को-কে (-ke)রামকে বলো Ramke bolo, tell Ram
में, पर-এ / -তে / -য় (-e / -te / -y)ঘরে ghore, in the room
से (from)থেকে (theke)দিল্লি থেকে Dilli theke, from Delhi
से (with, by means of)দিয়ে (diye)হাত দিয়ে hat diye, by hand
के साथসাথে / সঙ্গে (shathe / shongge)আমার সাথে amar shathe, with me
के लिएজন্য (jonno)তোমার জন্য tomar jonno, for you

There is no equivalent of the Hindi ने. "I ate" is simply আমি খেলাম (ami khelam); the subject does not change in the past.

5. "না" comes after the verb

Hindi: मैं नहीं जाता. Bangla: আমি যাই না (ami jai na). For the past, a different word is used: আমি যাইনি (ami jaini), "I did not go".

6. -টা and -জন: little words Hindi does not have

Bangla attaches a small marker to nouns and numbers. -টা (-ta), or the gentler -টি (-ti), marks a particular thing: বইটা (boita), "the book". With numbers it is required for things, and -জন (-jon) for people: তিনটে বই (tinte boi), three books; তিনজন ছাত্র (tinjon chhatro), three students. The noun stays singular after a number. Plurals for people use -রা (-ra): ছেলেরা (chhelera), the boys; for things, -গুলো (-gulo): বইগুলো, the books.

7. Tenses in one glance

Take করা (kora), to do, with আমি (I):

TenseBanglaRomanHindi
Simple presentআমি করিami koriमैं करता हूँ
Present continuousআমি করছিami korchhiमैं कर रहा हूँ
Present perfectআমি করেছিami korechhiमैंने किया है
Simple pastআমি করলামami korlamमैंने किया
Futureআমি করবami korboमैं करूँगा

The markers are easy to spot: -chh- for "-ing", -l- for the past, -b- for the future. The person ending follows them.

8. Asking questions

Question words begin with k, as in Hindi: কী ki (what), কে ke (who), কোথায় kothay (where), কখন kokhon (when), কেন keno (why), কেমন kemon (how), কত koto (how much). A yes-or-no question adds কি (ki) after the subject, where Hindi puts क्या first: তুমি কি আসবে? (tumi ki ashbe?), "Will you come?"

Make ten sentences

Use this frame with words from our word lists: [আমি] [place + -এ/-তে] [thing] [verb in -i]. আমি বাড়িতে ভাত খাই। (ami barite bhat khai), "I eat rice at home." Then turn each into a negative with না and a question with কি.
