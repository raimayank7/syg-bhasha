Indian kinship terms: why there is a word for every relative

English has one word, uncle, for the brother of your father, the brother of your mother, and the husband of an aunt. Hindi, Punjabi, Bengali and Urdu have a separate word for each, because the exact relationship has always mattered in family life. This guide explains the pattern.

The two sides of the family

Most terms depend on which side the relative is on. The father's side is called paternal and the mother's side maternal. In Hindi, a father's father is dada and a mother's father is nana. A father's brother is chacha (or tau if he is older than your father) and a mother's brother is mama.

Blood relatives and relatives by marriage

A father's sister is bua and her husband is phupha. A mother's sister is mausi and her husband is mausa. The wife of a mother's brother is mami. So the relationship goes through two steps, and the word shows both: the side, and whether the person is a blood relative or the spouse of one.

Elder and younger

Many languages also say whether a relative is older or younger than the person they are related through: the father's elder brother and the father's younger brother have different words in Hindi, Punjabi and Urdu (tau or taya, chacha). The chart on this site shows both.

How to read the chart

  • Each row is one relationship in English.
  • Each column is one language, shown in its own script.
  • A checked badge means the term was found in the English Wiktionary with a meaning that names that relationship. A single source badge means we could not confirm the term there, so use it with care and ask a speaker of the language.

Regional variety

The terms here are the widely used standard words. Families, regions and religions use their own variants: the same relative may be called by a different word in Haryana, in Lucknow or in Lahore, and Bengali families use different words for maternal and paternal grandparents than the ones given here. Nothing on this site is a rule of any community.

Questions

Why does Hindi have so many words for uncles and aunts?

Because the exact relationship matters in family life: the side of the family, whether the person is a blood relative, and whether they are older or younger than your parent.

Are these words the same in every region?

No. They are the common standard words. Families and regions have their own variants.
