How to address relatives: names, titles and respect

In many Indian families a younger person does not call an older relative by their name. They use the relationship word instead, often with a respectful ending.

Relationship word instead of the name

You say "Chacha" or "Mama", not the person's given name. If there are several, you add the name after the word: "Ramesh chacha", "Sunita mausi". This marks the relationship and respect at once.

Adding -ji

The ending -ji (जी) adds respect in Hindi, Punjabi and Urdu: "Mamaji", "Bhabhi ji", "Papa ji". Bengali uses other endings, such as -babu after a brother-in-law (jamaibabu) or -da and -di for elder brother and sister (dada, didi).

People who are not relatives

The same words are used for neighbours, family friends and shopkeepers: uncle and aunty (in English), bhaiya, didi, chacha, chachi. It is a way of showing that you treat them with a relative's respect.

Teaching children

  • Teach the words as a game, with a family photo: "Who is this? This is your mama, your mother's brother."
  • Start with the closest relatives (grandparents, aunts and uncles) and add in-laws when the child is older.
  • Say the word, not "uncle", every time, so that the child hears the difference between chacha and mama.

Names of relatives living abroad

Families living abroad often use English words (uncle, aunty, cousin) for convenience. If you want to keep the language, use this chart as a reminder and share it with the family.

Questions

Is it rude to call an older relative by name?

In many families, yes: elders are addressed by the relationship word, often with -ji, and not by their given name.

Why do people call a neighbour "uncle" or "aunty"?

To show respect and warmth. The words give the person a place in the family.
