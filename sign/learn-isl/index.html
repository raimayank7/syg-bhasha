How and where to learn ISL

You can begin ISL today, free, from official material. To become good at it you will also need people to sign with. This page lists the routes and suggests a plan.

1. ISLRTC's free online basic course

ISLRTC offers an Online Basic ISL Course in Self Learning Mode. Its page says the course aims to build basic communication skills in ISL and "awareness and respect for Deaf culture, etiquette, and norms", and that it is meant for groups such as government employees, educators, customer relation assistants, public-facing staff and content creators. It has ten modules, each with a quiz for self-assessment. The menu of topics, as listed on 12 November 2026:

  1. Let us greet in ISL
  2. Introduce yourself in ISL
  3. Most commonly used words in ISL, Part I
  4. Describing surrounding things (colours, shapes and size)
  5. Basic grammatical structure in ISL, Part I
  6. Most commonly used words in ISL, Part II
  7. Need based vocabulary, Part I
  8. Need based vocabulary, Part II
  9. Need based vocabulary, Part III
  10. Situation based conversation in ISL

This is the best starting point, because it teaches sentences and manners, not only single words.

2. The ISL Dictionary and the Sign Learn app

Use the ISL Dictionary beside the course, to look up words you need. ISLRTC's home page also links to its "Sign Learn" app for Android and iOS. See how to use the dictionary.

3. Classes and Deaf teachers

A language is learnt from its users. Look for:

  • Deaf associations and clubs in your city. Many run basic classes and welcome hearing learners who come with respect.
  • ISL teachers. ISLRTC publishes a directory of ISL teachers on its website, under Resources.
  • Schools for the deaf near you, which may know of classes for parents. ISLRTC has a directory of deaf schools.
  • Short training programmes. ISLRTC's news section reports basic ISL training held for different groups; watch its notices.

Prefer a class taught by a Deaf teacher. You learn the language as it is really used, and you learn the culture with it.

4. For school children

ISLRTC and NCERT have jointly developed ISL versions of NCERT textbooks for classes I to VI, placed on the DIKSHA portal (see NCERT Books in ISL). They are made for Deaf students, and they also let a hearing brother, sister or classmate see lessons in sign.

A twelve-week plan for a busy adult

  • Weeks 1 and 2: course modules 1 and 2. Learn greetings, to fingerspell your name, and to say that you are learning.
  • Weeks 3 to 5: modules 3 and 4, with the dictionary tutorials on people, food and everyday items. Fifteen minutes a day.
  • Weeks 6 and 7: module 5, the grammar. Slow down here; it is where ISL stops looking like English.
  • Weeks 8 to 11: modules 6 to 9. Choose the "need based" words for your own work: bank, clinic, classroom, shop.
  • Week 12: module 10, conversations. By now, find a signer to practise with.

Habits that work

  • Little and daily. Ten minutes every day beats two hours on Sunday. Hands need repetition.
  • Sign to a mirror or camera, and compare with the video.
  • Learn in pairs. A family member or colleague learning with you doubles your practice.
  • Keep your face alive. A blank face makes signing hard to read.
  • Tell Deaf people that you are a learner. Most will slow down and help.

What to avoid

  • Apps and videos that teach American or British signs. Check that the material says "Indian Sign Language".
  • Charts of hand drawings from unknown sources.
  • Believing that a few weeks make you an interpreter. Interpreting is a profession with a two-year diploma; see courses and careers.
