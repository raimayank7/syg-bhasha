ISLRTC: India's centre for sign language

The Indian Sign Language Research and Training Centre, ISLRTC, is the national body for Indian Sign Language. Its website describes it as an autonomous organisation under the Department of Empowerment of Persons with Disabilities (Divyangjan), Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment, Government of India, registered as a society, and "established on 28th September 2015". It is in New Delhi. Everything on this page is taken from ISLRTC's own website, read on 12 November 2026.

How it came about

ISLRTC's account of its own history is a story of persistence by the Deaf community:

  • In the 2000s the Indian deaf community asked for an institute for ISL teaching and research.
  • The 11th Five Year Plan (2007 to 2012) acknowledged that the needs of people with hearing disabilities had been relatively neglected, and envisaged a sign language research and training centre.
  • The setting up of ISLRTC was announced in the Union Budget speech of 2010-11.
  • In 2011 the centre was approved as an autonomous centre of the Indira Gandhi National Open University (IGNOU), Delhi, and its foundation stone was laid on the IGNOU campus on 4 October 2011. In 2013 the centre at IGNOU was closed.
  • In April 2015 the Ministry decided to merge it with a regional centre of another institute. The Deaf community protested, because the two bodies had different outlooks and goals.
  • After protests and meetings, the Union Cabinet approved ISLRTC as a society on 22 September 2015, and the order was issued on 28 September 2015.

Why it was needed

The same page explains the gap: ISL was used by the deaf community all over India but not used in deaf schools to teach deaf children; teacher training did not prepare teachers to use ISL; there was no teaching material that included sign language; parents of deaf children did not know about it; and, in ISLRTC's words, India had "only less than 300 certified interpreters".

Its objectives

ISLRTC lists six objectives. In short:

  1. To develop people who can use, teach and research ISL, including bilingualism.
  2. To promote ISL as a mode of education for deaf students at primary, secondary and higher levels.
  3. To do research with universities in India and abroad and to create linguistic records of ISL, including a corpus of its vocabulary.
  4. To orient and train government officials, teachers, professionals, community leaders and the public to understand and use ISL.
  5. To work with organisations of the deaf and others in the disability field to promote ISL.
  6. To gather information on sign languages used elsewhere in the world.

What it offers

  • The ISL Dictionary, as a website, on YouTube, as downloadable files and as an open dataset. See how to use the ISL Dictionary.
  • A free online basic ISL course in self-learning mode, in ten modules. See how to learn ISL.
  • Two diploma courses, for interpreters and for Deaf teachers of ISL. See courses and careers.
  • ISL versions of NCERT textbooks for classes I to VI, developed jointly with NCERT and placed on the DIKSHA portal.
  • Directories of ISL interpreters, ISL teachers and schools for the deaf.
  • Educational concept videos, a newsletter and annual reports.
  • A Video Relay Service, listed on its home page with weekday office hours.

Phone numbers, admission dates and fees change, so we do not copy them here. Take them from islrtc.nic.in on the day you need them.
