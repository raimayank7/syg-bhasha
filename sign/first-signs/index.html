First signs to learn: a checklist

120 everyday words in six groups. Look each one up in the official video dictionary, practise it, and tick it here.

How this page works. We do not show the signs. For each word, open the official ISL Dictionary, search for the word (or browse its category), watch the video, and copy the sign in front of a mirror. Then tick the word. Your ticks are saved on this device only. A few words may be listed in the dictionary under a slightly different word; try a near word if a search finds nothing.

Why these words

The six groups follow the beginner section of the official dictionary website, "Tutorial of most commonly used words in Indian Sign Language", which has greetings, people and relations, food, daily routines, everyday items and mixed words. Within each group we chose the words a hearing learner needs in the first weeks at home, in a classroom or at a counter. The list is ours; the signs are the dictionary's.

A way to use the list

  1. Five words a day. Take them from one group, so that they help each other.
  2. Review before adding. Sign yesterday's five from memory before today's.
  3. Make tiny sentences. As soon as you know "mother", "eat" and "finish", put them together and ask a signer whether the order is right. ISL has its own word order.
  4. Untick what you forget. A tick should mean "I can sign this without looking it up".
  5. After the first group, start the free basic course alongside. Words without grammar are a pile of bricks.

Three sentences to learn early

From the first group, learn these as whole phrases, because they make every later conversation easier: "I am learning sign language", "Please sign slowly" and "Please repeat". Deaf people meet many hearing people who know no signs at all. A learner who can say these three is nearly always met with patience and help.
