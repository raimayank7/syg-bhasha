Fingerspelling in ISL

Fingerspelling means showing the letters of a written word, one by one, with the hands. Every sign language that lives beside a written language has a way of doing this. In ISL it is used for the letters of the English alphabet.

Why there is no alphabet chart on this page

You may have come here for a picture of the ISL alphabet. We decided not to draw one, and we want to say plainly why. A hand shape is a three-dimensional thing: the angle of the wrist, which finger touches which, and the direction of the palm all matter. We could not produce drawings of all twenty-six letters that we were sure were correct, and a chart with even two wrong letters teaches a mistake to everyone who prints it. Charts found by an image search are also very often of the American one-handed alphabet, which is a different system.

So this page explains fingerspelling in words and sends you to videos made by the national centre. Use the "Search By Alphabet" option of the official ISL Dictionary and the opening modules of the free basic ISL course, which begin with greetings and introducing yourself.

What to know before you start

  • ISL fingerspelling is generally two-handed. Most letters are formed by the two hands together. The American alphabet, which many apps teach, uses one hand. If a chart shows every letter on a single hand, it is not the ISL alphabet.
  • It spells English letters. Names written in Hindi or other Indian scripts are usually spelt through their English spelling.
  • It is a small part of signing. Fluent signers fingerspell only when they need to.

When signers fingerspell

  • Names of people on first meeting. After that, Deaf people often give a person a "name sign", a single sign that stands for that person.
  • Names of places, brands and titles that have no sign of their own.
  • New or technical words for which a sign is not yet in common use.
  • To make something clear when a sign is not understood.

They do not fingerspell ordinary words that have signs. Spelling out a whole sentence letter by letter is slow and tiring to read, a little like someone spelling aloud every word they say.

How to learn the letters well

  1. Learn from video, in small groups. Take five letters a day, in the order of the alphabet.
  2. Learn your own name first. You will use it in every first conversation.
  3. Keep your hands steady in front of your chest. Do not bounce them with each letter.
  4. Aim for rhythm, not speed. Even, clear letters are easier to read than fast ones.
  5. Say the word, not the letters, in your head as you spell. It helps the letters flow into one shape.
  6. Pause briefly between words and, for a double letter, repeat the shape with a small move.
  7. Practise reading as well as making. Reading someone else's fingerspelling is the harder skill. Ask a partner to spell short words for you.

Words to practise on

Once you know the letters, practise with words that matter to you:

  • your first name, then your full name
  • the names of your family
  • your town and your state
  • your school, college or workplace
  • the days of the week

Numbers

ISL has signs for numbers too. Number systems differ between sign languages, so again learn them from the official videos and from signers near you, not from a chart made for another country.

After the alphabet

Do not stay on fingerspelling for long. Within a week, move to real signs: greetings, family words, food and everyday things. The first signs checklist gives an order to follow.
