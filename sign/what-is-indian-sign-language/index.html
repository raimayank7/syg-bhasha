Sign Language

What is Indian Sign Language?

Indian Sign Language, or ISL, is the language of the Deaf community of India. ISLRTC, the Government of India's centre for the language, describes it as "a visual manual language used by the Deaf community in India" with "distinct grammar and vocabulary". It is seen, not heard: meaning is carried by the shape and movement of the hands, by where they are placed, and by the face, head and body.

A language, not a code

The most common mistake hearing people make is to think that sign language is Hindi or English "spelt out on the hands". It is not. ISL is a language in its own right.

  • It has its own words. A sign stands for an idea, not for the letters of a spoken word.
  • It has its own grammar. The order of signs in a sentence is not the order of words in English or Hindi. Space is part of the grammar: a signer can place a person or a thing at a spot in the air and point back to it later, the way a speaker uses "he" or "it".
  • The face is part of the sentence. Raised eyebrows, a tilt of the head or a shake of the head can turn a statement into a question or a "no". These are grammar, not emotion.
  • It can say anything. Jokes, poetry, arguments, science lessons and news are all signed.

Who uses it

ISLRTC states that ISL "is used in the deaf community all over India", and that it is a means of communication for Deaf people and also for "teachers, parents, siblings, and interpreters". On its About page, ISLRTC cites the 2011 Census figure of about 50 lakh deaf persons in India. Not every deaf person signs, and many hearing people do: children of Deaf parents, interpreters, teachers and friends.

Is it the same all over India?

ISL is used across the country, with regional differences in some signs, much as a spoken language has dialects. Signers from different states generally understand each other. One aim of ISLRTC's dictionary work is to record the vocabulary of the language so that learners, teachers and interpreters everywhere have a common reference.

Is it the same as American or British Sign Language?

No. There is no single sign language of the world. American Sign Language, British Sign Language, ISL and many others are separate languages that grew in their own communities. A video that teaches American signs will not teach you ISL. This matters, because most sign language videos and apps found by a casual search are American.

Fingerspelling is only a small part

Signers can spell a word letter by letter with the hands, which is useful for names. Learners often start there and think they are learning the language. Fingerspelling is to ISL what spelling aloud is to English: a tool, not the language. See fingerspelling in ISL.

What ISL is not

  • It is not mime or gesture that anyone can guess. Signs must be learnt, like words.
  • It is not a simplified or broken form of a spoken language.
  • It is not "Signed Hindi" or "Signed English", in which a spoken sentence is shown word by word with signs. Those are teaching systems, not the natural language of the community.

Why learn it

For a Deaf child it is the difference between having a full first language and not having one. For a family it is how dinner-table talk includes everyone. For a teacher, a doctor's receptionist, a bank clerk or a shopkeeper, even fifty signs change how a Deaf person is received. The next step is how and where to learn ISL.
