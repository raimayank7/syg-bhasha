Sign Language

How to use the official ISL Dictionary

The single most useful free resource for a learner is the Indian Sign Language Dictionary made by ISLRTC. Every entry is a short video of a signer showing the sign. Because a sign is a movement, video is the right way to show it, and it is why this site sends you there instead of printing drawings.

Where it is

  • Dictionary website: divyangjan.depwd.gov.in/islrtc, on the website of the Department of Empowerment of Persons with Disabilities.
  • On YouTube and as files: ISLRTC's website links to the dictionary on its YouTube channel and to a shared folder for download. Use the links under "ISL Dictionary" in the menu of islrtc.nic.in.
  • As open data: a dictionary dataset is listed on the Government's open data portal, data.gov.in.
  • As an app: ISLRTC's home page links to a "Sign Learn" app for Android and for iOS.

Three ways to search

The dictionary website offers three search modes (as seen on 12 November 2026):

  1. Normal Search. Type a word in English or Hindi to find its sign.
  2. Search By Alphabet. Browse the words that begin with a chosen letter.
  3. Search By Category. Browse by subject, in English or Hindi. This is the best mode for learning, because you meet related words together.

The beginner tutorials

The same page has a section called "Tutorial of most commonly used words in Indian Sign Language", with six groups:

  • Greetings and communicative phrases
  • People and relations
  • Food related
  • Daily routines and everyday items
  • Everyday items
  • Mixed words

Start here. Our first signs checklist follows the same groups, so you can tick off words as you learn them.

How to study a sign from a video

  1. Watch once without moving. Look at the whole signer, not only the hands.
  2. Watch again for four things: the shape of the hand, where it is placed, how it moves, and which way the palm faces.
  3. Watch the face. Expression and head movement are part of many signs.
  4. Copy it slowly, in front of a mirror or your phone's front camera. Remember that the signer in the video faces you: their right hand is on your left.
  5. Repeat it in a sentence. A sign learnt alone is forgotten in a day.
  6. Check with a signer whenever you can. A Deaf person will correct in a second what a video cannot.

Which hand?

Signers have a stronger hand, usually the one they write with, which does most of the movement. If you are left-handed, sign with your left hand as the active one and keep to it. Do not swap from sign to sign.

Limits to keep in mind

  • A dictionary gives words, not grammar. Stringing dictionary signs together in English word order does not make ISL. For sentences, take the basic course or a class.
  • Signs vary a little by region. The sign you learn may differ from the one used by a Deaf person in your town. Use theirs with them.
  • The website's own note says that its translations were provided by an external agency and invites corrections by email. Like any dictionary, it is improved over time.

We have not counted the entries and do not state a number of words here, because the collection grows.
