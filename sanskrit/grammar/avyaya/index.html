Avyaya: the words that never change

After the tables of nouns and verbs, here is a restful page. An अव्यय (avyaya, "unchanging") is a word that keeps one form always. It has no gender, no number and no case. Sanskrit's small words for "and", "not", "here", "now", "also" and "but" are all avyayas, and since they appear in nearly every sentence, learning fifty of them pays off quickly.

They are especially useful for reading verse. In a shloka, the avyayas are fixed landmarks. Find च, न, एव, अपि, तु, हि, and the changing words around them begin to sort themselves out.

The matching pairs

Sanskrit loves sentences built on a pair: a word beginning with य- in the first half answered by a word beginning with त- in the second. Hindi inherited the habit: जब... तब, जहाँ... वहाँ, जैसा... वैसा.

  • यत्र... तत्र: where... there. यत्र धूमः तत्र अग्निः। Where there is smoke, there is fire.
  • यदा... तदा: when... then. The Gita's यदा यदा हि धर्मस्य... तदात्मानं सृजाम्यहम् is built on this pair.
  • यथा... तथा: as... so. यथा राजा तथा प्रजा। As the king, so the people.
  • यावत्... तावत्: as long as... so long.

Small words in the shlokas

Look at how many of the words in a single Gita line are avyayas: मा फलेषु कदाचन is "never (मा... कदाचन) in the fruits". न हि ज्ञानेन सदृशं पवित्रम् इह विद्यते: "not indeed (न हि) ... here (इह)". Because these words never change, you can recognise them from your first week, long before the noun and verb tables are secure. That is a real encouragement when a verse looks impenetrable.

Two cautions

  • च ("and") comes after the word it adds, not between: रामः सीता च, "Rama and Sita".
  • न is "not" for statements; मा is "do not" for prohibitions, as in मा शुचः, "do not grieve".
