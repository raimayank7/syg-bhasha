Family and body

25 words in Sanskrit, each with a roman reading and the meaning in Hindi and English.

The Sanskrit words for family and body are the ancestors of the Hindi ones, and seeing them side by side is a lesson in how language changes. माता became माँ, भ्राता became भाई, हस्तः became हाथ, दन्तः became दाँत, कर्णः became कान. Words that kept their Sanskrit form in Hindi are called तत्सम; words that changed are called तद्भव. School Hindi papers ask for exactly these pairs.

Each word is given in the first case, singular, which is the form you would use as the doer in a sentence. The ending shows the gender: -ः is usually masculine, -आ or -ई feminine, -म् neuter. The words for relations ending in -ता (माता, पिता, भ्राता) come from stems in -तृ and have their own table; you will meet पितृ and मातृ in compounds such as पितृभक्तिः and मातृभूमिः.

Use them in sentences

  • मम माता शिक्षिका अस्ति। My mother is a teacher.
  • मम द्वौ हस्तौ स्तः। I have two hands. (Note the dual: हस्तौ, and the dual verb स्तः.)
  • अहं नेत्राभ्यां पश्यामि। I see with my (two) eyes. The third case, dual, shows the means.
  • अहं कर्णाभ्यां शृणोमि। I hear with my ears.

मित्रम् (friend) is neuter in Sanskrit even when the friend is a man, a small surprise that examiners like to ask about.
