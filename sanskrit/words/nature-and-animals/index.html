Nature, animals and food

26 words in Sanskrit, each with a roman reading and the meaning in Hindi and English.

Sanskrit is famously rich in words for the natural world. The dictionary of synonyms composed by Amarasimha, the Amarakosha, lists many names for the sun, the moon, water, fire and the lotus, each showing the thing from a different side. सूर्यः is the sun; so are रविः, भानुः, दिनकरः ("day-maker") and भास्करः ("light-maker"). Many Indian personal names come from this store.

This page keeps to one everyday word for each thing, the one taught first in school. Most will be familiar from Hindi, either unchanged (जलम्, वृक्षः, पुष्पम्, फलम्) or in a worn-down form: मयूरः gave मोर, आम्रम् gave आम, दुग्धम् gave दूध, काकः gave कौआ.

As a rule of thumb, names of fruits are neuter (आम्रम्, कदलीफलम्) while the trees that bear them are masculine (आम्रः, the mango tree).

Use them in sentences

  • सूर्यः पूर्वदिशि उदेति। The sun rises in the east.
  • वृक्षे खगाः वसन्ति। Birds live in the tree.
  • गौः दुग्धं ददाति। The cow gives milk.
  • मयूरः भारतस्य राष्ट्रियः पक्षी अस्ति। The peacock is the national bird of India.
  • अहम् आम्रं खादामि। I eat a mango.

गौः (cow) has an irregular table of its own; its commonest other forms are गाम् (second case) and गवि (seventh case), which appears in Gita 5.18 on our knowledge page.
