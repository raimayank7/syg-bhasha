Days, colours and school

25 words in Sanskrit, each with a roman reading and the meaning in Hindi and English.

The Sanskrit days of the week are the Hindi ones with वासरः ("day") in place of वार: रविवासरः, सोमवासरः. Each is named after a heavenly body, in the same order used across India and, remarkably, across Europe too: the Sun, the Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus and Saturn. गुरुः is the planet Jupiter and also "teacher", which is why Thursday is the teacher's day.

Colour words are adjectives, so they take the gender of the thing they describe: a white cloth is श्वेतं वस्त्रम् (neuter), a white horse श्वेतः अश्वः (masculine), a white cow श्वेता गौः (feminine). They are given here in the masculine form.

The school words at the end let you name everything on your desk. Several were coined in modern times from old roots, in the way Sanskrit has always made new words: सङ्गणकम् (computer) is "that which counts together", and दूरवाणी (telephone) is "far voice".

Use them in sentences

  • अद्य सोमवासरः अस्ति। Today is Monday.
  • श्वः मङ्गलवासरः भविष्यति। Tomorrow will be Tuesday.
  • आकाशः नीलः अस्ति। The sky is blue.
  • मम लेखनी रक्ता अस्ति। My pen is red. (लेखनी is feminine, so "red" becomes रक्ता.)
  • छात्राः कक्षायां पठन्ति। The students read in the classroom.
