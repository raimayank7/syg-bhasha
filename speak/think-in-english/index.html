From Hindi to English: stop translating, start building

If you make every English sentence in Hindi first and then translate it, you will speak slowly and make the same mistakes for years. The two languages build sentences differently. Learn the five main differences below and you will begin to build English sentences directly.

1. The verb moves to the middle

Hindi and Punjabi put the verb at the end: मैं चाय पीता हूँ (I tea drink). English puts it right after the doer: I drink tea. The order is doer, action, thing: S + V + O.

  • राम ने सेब खाया। → Ram ate an apple.
  • ਮੈਂ ਕਿਤਾਬ ਪੜ੍ਹਦਾ ਹਾਂ। → I read a book.

So in English, decide the action early. Say the doer, say the verb, and the rest follows.

2. Small words come before, not after

Hindi has postpositions: मेज़ पर, घर में, दिल्ली से. English has prepositions, placed before the noun: on the table, in the house, from Delhi.

3. English needs "a" and "the"

Hindi has no articles, so learners drop them: "I bought book." English needs one almost every time a countable thing is named: I bought a book (any one), I read the book you gave me (that one). When in doubt before a single countable thing, an article is probably needed.

4. One "you", one "he"

Hindi has तू, तुम, आप; English has only "you". Respect is shown by other words: please, could you, sir, ma'am. English also does not use a plural for respect: say "My father is a teacher. He is at home", not "They are at home".

5. The helping verb carries the tense

Hindi changes the end of the verb. English often adds a separate helper: do, does, did, is, are, was, will, have. It is the helper that moves to make a question and takes "not" to make a negative.

  • He likes tea. → Does he like tea? → He does not like tea.
  • They went home. → Did they go home? → They did not go home.

How to start thinking in English

  1. Name things. As you walk through the house, name what you see in English: door, tap, switch, bucket. If a word is missing, look it up that evening.
  2. Describe what you are doing. Silently, in tiny sentences: "I am opening the door. I am filling the bottle." This is the continuous tense, the easiest to begin with.
  3. Use ready-made pieces. Fluent speakers do not build every sentence from single words. They join pieces: "Could you please...", "I would like to...", "Do you mind if...". Our sentence patterns page gives twenty such pieces.
  4. Allow short sentences. Two short correct sentences are better than one long broken one. "I was late. The bus did not come." is good English.
  5. Do not stop to hunt for a word. Go around it. If you forget "ladle", say "the big spoon for dal". Native speakers do this too.

A five-minute drill

Take any five Hindi sentences you said today. For each, do not translate. Ask three questions: Who did it? What did they do? To what, where or when? Then say the English in that order. "कल मैंने बाज़ार से सब्ज़ी ख़रीदी": Who? I. Did what? Bought. What, where, when? Vegetables, from the market, yesterday. "I bought vegetables from the market yesterday."
