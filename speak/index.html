    Who this site is for

    It is for people who studied English at school, can read it, and still freeze when they have to speak: the student facing an interview, the shopkeeper with an English-speaking customer, the parent at a school meeting, the worker moving to a big city. If you think in Hindi or Punjabi, the meanings under each sentence are there to help you, and the guides explain exactly where our languages and English part ways.

    How to use it

    1. Pick one situation you will really face this week.
    2. Listen and repeat each sentence aloud. Reading silently does not train the tongue.
    3. Make it yours: change the name, place or thing so that the sentence is true for you.
    4. Use two sentences with a real person the same day.
    5. Read one guide a week: begin with thinking in English and common mistakes.

    Our approach

    We teach standard, simple English in short sentences, with Indian names, places and daily life. We do not ask you to copy a foreign accent; a clear Indian accent is fine anywhere. We point out, without mocking, the forms that are common in India but confusing elsewhere, so that you can choose.

    For written English, our neighbour Formats has letters and applications to copy.

    Common questions

    Can I learn spoken English at home without a class?

    Yes. Speaking improves with daily speaking, not with attendance. Fifteen to twenty minutes aloud every day, using the situation pages and the practice routine here, is enough to make clear progress in a month.

    Do I need to learn all the grammar first?

    No. Start speaking with ready sentences, and learn the five tenses and the question patterns alongside. Grammar makes more sense once you have sentences in your mouth to attach it to.

    How does the Listen button work?

    It uses the text-to-speech voice built into your phone or computer. If you hear nothing, your device may have no English voice installed, or the sound may be muted.

    Is the meaning given in Punjabi too?

    Yes. Every sentence on the situation pages has its meaning in Hindi and in Punjabi (Gurmukhi). You can show one or both.
