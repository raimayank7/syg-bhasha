How to practise spoken English alone, 15 minutes a day

Speaking is a physical skill, like cycling. Reading about it helps a little; doing it daily helps a lot. The usual excuse is "I have nobody to speak English with". You do not need anybody for the first three months. You need fifteen minutes and a phone.

The 15 minute routine

MinutesWhat to doWhy
3Listen. One situation page here, with the Listen button, or a short news clip.The ear must know the sound before the mouth can make it.
4Shadow. Play a sentence, pause, and say it exactly as you heard it. Ten sentences.Copies rhythm and stress, which rules cannot teach.
4Self-talk. Describe what you did today, or what you see, aloud.Forces you to build your own sentences.
2Record. Speak for one minute on any subject into your phone.You become your own teacher.
2Review. Listen to the recording; note one mistake and one missing word.One fix a day is 365 fixes a year.

Shadowing, step by step

  1. Choose a sentence, for example "Could you tell me the way to the bus stand?"
  2. Listen once without speaking.
  3. Listen again and speak along with it, like singing along with a song.
  4. Say it alone, at the same speed, without looking.
  5. Change one word and say it again: "...the way to the post office?"

Shadowing feels silly for two days and natural after a week. It is the single most effective exercise for a learner without a partner.

Subjects for self-talk and recording

Running out of things to say is the usual reason people stop. Keep this list: my morning; what I ate today; my family; my best friend; my town; a film I saw; my work or my school; a festival I like; what I will do tomorrow; a problem I solved; my favourite food and how it is made; a place I want to visit. One subject a day lasts twelve days; then begin again and notice how much more you can say.

When you get stuck on a word

Do not stop and do not switch to Hindi. Describe the thing in other words and carry on. After the minute is over, look up the word and write it in a small notebook. That notebook becomes your personal dictionary, made only of the words you actually needed.

Finding people, when you are ready

  • At home: fix an "English half hour" at dinner. Mistakes are allowed; laughing at them is not.
  • With a friend: a ten minute phone call in English every evening, on a fixed subject.
  • At work or college: start with greetings and small requests in English; they are low-risk.
  • With children: read a picture book aloud to them in English. They are the kindest audience.

What slows people down

  • Waiting to be perfect. Speak with mistakes. Correct speech grows out of incorrect speech, not out of silence.
  • Only watching videos. Listening without speaking gives understanding, not fluency.
  • Learning big words. Daily conversation uses about two thousand simple words. Use the small ones well.
  • Practising once a week. Fifteen minutes daily beats two hours on Sunday.

How to know you are improving

Keep your first recording. Record the same subject again after thirty days and listen to both. The second will be longer, smoother and have fewer "umm"s. That comparison does more for your confidence than any certificate.
