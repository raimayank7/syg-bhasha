Why learn by situation?

Nobody speaks grammar. People speak in places: at the counter, on the phone, at the door. In each place the same few sentences come up again and again. If you learn the twenty sentences of the market, you can shop in English next week, long before you have finished any grammar book. That early success is what keeps a learner going.

How each page is built

  1. A short introduction that explains the one or two language points that matter most in that place, such as "since" and "for" at the doctor's.
  2. The sentences, each with its meaning in Hindi and Punjabi. Choose to see Hindi, Punjabi or both; the site remembers your choice. The Listen button reads the sentence with the English voice of your device, and Copy puts it on your clipboard.
  3. A dialogue of five or six lines, so that you hear the sentences answer each other.
  4. "Speak it right": three common slips in that situation and the standard form.

Which page first?

Start with greetings and polite words; they are used everywhere. Then take the place you actually go to most: students to school, working people to the office, homemakers to the market and guests. Job seekers should read the interview page together with common mistakes.

One page a day

A page takes about fifteen minutes when done aloud. The 30 day plan puts all the pages in order, with one small real-life task each day.
