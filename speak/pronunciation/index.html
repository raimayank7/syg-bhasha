English pronunciation for Hindi and Punjabi speakers

You do not need a foreign accent. An Indian accent that is clear is understood all over the world. What you do need is to keep apart the few sounds that change the meaning of a word. This page covers the ones that trouble Hindi and Punjabi speakers most, with words to practise. Use the Listen buttons on the situation pages to hear full sentences.

1. V and W

Hindi has one sound, व, for both. English has two. For v, the upper teeth touch the lower lip: very, van, vote, seven. For w, the lips make a small circle and the teeth touch nothing: we, water, window, away.

Practise: vest / west, vine / wine, veil / whale. "We visited a very wide village."

2. Z, not J

Many speakers say "jero" for zero and "jip" for zip. Z is the sound of ज़ in ज़रूर: a buzzing "s". Say "sss", then switch on your voice: "zzz".

Practise: zoo, zero, zip, busy, easy, lazy, these, please. Remember that the letter "s" is often said as z: is, his, was, has, days.

3. The two "th" sounds

In "think" and "three", English has a soft sound made with the tongue lightly between the teeth, closer to थ than ट. In "this" and "mother", it is closer to द than ड. Avoid the hard ट and ड: "tree" and "three" are different words.

Practise: thin / tin, thank / tank, they / day, then / den.

4. S and SH; the extra vowel before "s"

Keep "s" and "sh" apart: sip / ship, sea / she, same / shame. And do not add a vowel before words that begin with s + another consonant: it is "school", "station", "street", not "ischool" or "sakool". Start with a long "sss" and slide straight into the next sound: sss-kool.

5. F and PH

English f is made with teeth on the lip, as in फ़ of फ़ोन, not the फ of फल: fan, five, coffee, office. "Ph" in English spelling is also said f: phone, photo.

6. Short and long vowels

The length of a vowel can change the word.

Also keep apart the vowel of "pen" (ए, short) and "pan" (ऐ): men / man, bed / bad, set / sat. And the vowel of "cot" (short) and "coat" (long): not / note, got / goat.

7. Silent letters

English spelling keeps letters that are no longer spoken. Common ones: the k in know, knee, knife; the w in write, wrong, answer; the b in comb, thumb, doubt; the l in walk, talk, half, calm; the t in listen, castle, often (many speakers); the h in hour, honest; the p in receipt. "Wednesday" is said "wenz-day", and "vegetable" has three beats: "vej-tuh-bl".

8. Word stress

Hindi gives nearly equal weight to every syllable. English makes one syllable of each long word louder and longer, and weakens the others. Stressing the wrong one is a bigger cause of not being understood than any single sound.

  • First syllable: TA-ble, HOS-pi-tal, IN-ter-est-ing, CAL-en-dar
  • Second syllable: ho-TEL, de-VEL-op, be-GIN, im-POR-tant
  • Third syllable: un-der-STAND, en-gi-NEER

Some words change meaning with stress: a PRE-sent (gift), to pre-SENT (give); a RE-cord, to re-CORD.

How to practise

  1. Take one pair of sounds a week, not all at once.
  2. Say the practice words slowly in front of a mirror and watch your lips and teeth.
  3. Record ten seconds of yourself on your phone and listen. You will hear what a teacher would hear.
  4. Listen to a news reader for five minutes and repeat sentence by sentence, copying the stress.

This page uses Hindi letters as a rough guide to sounds. No two languages match exactly; a good dictionary with audio is the final check for any single word.
