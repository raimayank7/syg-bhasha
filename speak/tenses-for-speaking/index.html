The five tenses you really need for speaking

Grammar books list twelve tenses. Daily conversation runs mostly on five. Learn these five well, with their questions and negatives, before you worry about the rest. For each one, the Hindi ending that signals it is given, because that is how most of us recognise which tense we need.

1. Simple present: habits and facts

Hindi signal: ता है, ती है, ते हैं (वह रोज़ स्कूल जाता है).

Pattern: I/you/we/they go; he/she/it goes. The "s" on he/she/it is the most forgotten letter in spoken English.

  • I get up at six. She works in a bank. The shop opens at nine.
  • Question: Do you get up at six? Does she work in a bank?
  • Negative: I do not get up at six. She does not work in a bank.

2. Present continuous: happening now

Hindi signal: रहा है, रही है, रहे हैं (वह खाना बना रही है).

Pattern: am / is / are + verb-ing.

  • I am cooking. He is sleeping. They are playing outside.
  • Question: Are you cooking? Is he sleeping?
  • Negative: I am not cooking. He is not sleeping.

It is also used for fixed plans: "We are leaving tomorrow morning."

3. Simple past: finished, at a known time

Hindi signal: या, ई, ए, था (मैंने कल फ़िल्म देखी).

Pattern: verb + ed (watched, played) or the second form (went, saw, ate, bought).

  • I watched a film yesterday. We went to Amritsar last week.
  • Question: Did you watch the film? Did you go to Amritsar?
  • Negative: I did not watch it. We did not go.

After "did", the verb returns to its first form: "Did you go?", never "Did you went?"

4. Present perfect: done, and it matters now

Hindi signal: चुका है, लिया है, दिया है (मैंने खाना खा लिया है).

Pattern: have / has + third form (eaten, done, seen, gone).

  • I have finished my work. She has gone to the market. Have you had lunch?
  • Negative: I have not finished. She has not come yet.

The rule that saves most mistakes: if you say when (yesterday, last year, in 2020), use the simple past, not this tense. "I have seen him yesterday" is wrong; "I saw him yesterday" is right.

5. Simple future: will and going to

Hindi signal: गा, गी, गे (मैं कल आऊँगा).

Pattern: will + first form, or am/is/are going to + first form.

  • I will call you tonight. It is going to rain. We are going to buy a new fridge.
  • Question: Will you come? Are you going to tell him?
  • Negative: I will not (won't) be late.

Use "will" for a decision made now or a promise; use "going to" for a plan already made or something you can see coming.

One table to keep

Practice

Take one verb a day, for example "eat", and say all fifteen sentences of the table with it about your own day: "I eat roti. Do I eat rice? I do not eat meat..." It takes two minutes and trains the mouth as well as the mind.
