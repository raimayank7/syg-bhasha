Nothing ventured, nothing gained

If you do not take a risk, you cannot win.

What it means

If you do not take a risk, you cannot win.

An example

Apply for the job; nothing ventured, nothing gained.

When to use it

Use it when the situation matches this idea: if you do not take a risk, you cannot win. Say it lightly and only once in a conversation or a piece of writing: read using proverbs well for more. It has 4 words.

How it sounds

Speakers usually say it as encouragement. It has two halves separated by a pause, and the second half answers the first, which gives it a rhythm that is easy to repeat. Its longest word is "ventured," (9 letters).

The picture inside it

Most proverbs put an idea into a small scene. Here the scene is a trader who never sails and so never brings home anything. When you meet the saying, picture that scene and then ask how the situation in front of you is like it.

Another example

Apply for the scholarship anyway; nothing ventured, nothing gained.

A saying that pulls the other way

  • Better safe than sorryOne praises taking a risk, the other praises caution.
  • A rolling stone gathers no mossOne doubts a life of moving, the other rewards trying new things.

Proverbs often contradict each other. That is not a mistake: each fits a different situation, and a good speaker picks the one that fits.

Try it yourself

Write one sentence of your own that ends with "nothing ventured, nothing gained", about something that really happened to you or a person you know. If the meaning, "if you do not take a risk, you cannot win", fits your story, you have understood the proverb.

It is a proverb: a complete short sentence that gives advice or states a general truth.
