Better safe than sorry

It is wiser to be careful than to regret it later.

What it means

It is wiser to be careful than to regret it later.

An example

Take an umbrella; better safe than sorry.

When to use it

Use it when the situation matches this idea: it is wiser to be careful than to regret it later. Say it lightly and only once in a conversation or a piece of writing: read using proverbs well for more. It has 4 words.

How it sounds

Speakers usually say it as advice. It is built as a comparison ("better ... than ..."), a pattern that makes the advice easy to remember. Its longest word is "better" (6 letters).

The picture inside it

Most proverbs put an idea into a small scene. Here the scene is a person who takes an umbrella on a cloudy day. When you meet the saying, picture that scene and then ask how the situation in front of you is like it.

Another example

Take a copy of the file; better safe than sorry.

A saying that pulls the other way

  • Nothing ventured, nothing gainedOne praises taking a risk, the other praises caution.

Proverbs often contradict each other. That is not a mistake: each fits a different situation, and a good speaker picks the one that fits.

Try it yourself

Write one sentence of your own that ends with "better safe than sorry", about something that really happened to you or a person you know. If the meaning, "it is wiser to be careful than to regret it later", fits your story, you have understood the proverb.

Questions

Is "better safe than sorry" a proverb or an idiom?

It is a proverb: a complete short sentence that gives advice or states a general truth.
