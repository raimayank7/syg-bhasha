Por or para: how to choose between the two prepositions

Both por and para often translate as "for", which is why they confuse learners. In general, para points forward to a goal, and por looks back at a cause or at the way something happens.

Para: goal and direction

  • Purpose (in order to): "Estudio para aprender." (I study to learn.)
  • Recipient: "El regalo es para ti." (The gift is for you.)
  • Destination: "Salgo para Madrid." (I leave for Madrid.)
  • Deadline: "Lo necesito para el lunes." (I need it by Monday.)
  • Opinion or comparison: "Para mí, es fácil." (For me, it is easy.)

Por: cause, exchange and way

  • Reason: "Lo hago por ti." (I do it because of you, for your sake.)
  • Exchange: "Pagué diez euros por el libro." (I paid ten euros for the book.)
  • Means: "Hablamos por teléfono." (We speak by phone.)
  • Through, along, around: "Caminamos por el parque."
  • Duration: "Trabajé por tres horas." (I worked for three hours.)
  • Passive agent: "La carta fue escrita por ella."

A quick test

Ask yourself: is there a goal ahead (para), or a cause, a route or an exchange (por)? If you can say "in order to" in English, use para.

Check yourself

  1. Este libro es (por / para) mi madre. Answer: para.
  2. Gracias (por / para) tu ayuda. Answer: por.
  3. Salimos (por / para) la playa mañana. Answer: para (destination).

The verbs in these examples can be conjugated in the conjugator.

Questions

Can por and para both be right?

Sometimes, with a change of meaning: "trabajo por ti" means I work in your place or for your sake, while "trabajo para ti" means I work for you, as an employee.

Which one is used with a deadline?

Para: "Lo necesito para el viernes."
