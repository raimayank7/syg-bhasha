Ser or estar: two verbs for "to be"

Spanish has two verbs that both mean "to be". Choosing between them is one of the first hard points for learners. A simple rule of thumb helps: ser says what something is, and estar says where it is or how it is at the moment.

Use ser for

  • Identity and description: "Ella es doctora." (She is a doctor.) "El libro es grande." (The book is big.)
  • Origin and nationality: "Somos de México." (We are from Mexico.)
  • Material and ownership: "La mesa es de madera." "Es mi casa."
  • Time and date: "Son las tres." "Hoy es lunes."
  • Events: "La fiesta es en mi casa." (an event takes place with ser, even though it is a place)

Use estar for

  • Location: "Estoy en casa." "Madrid está en España."
  • States and moods, often temporary: "Estamos cansados." "La sopa está fría."
  • Actions in progress: "Están comiendo." (estar plus the gerund)
  • Results: "La puerta está abierta."

Adjectives that change meaning

Some adjectives take either verb and change meaning: ser aburrido is to be boring, estar aburrido is to be bored; ser listo is to be clever, estar listo is to be ready; ser malo is to be bad, estar malo is to be ill.

Check yourself

  1. La reunión (es / está) a las cinco. Answer: es (time of an event).
  2. Mi hermano (es / está) en Madrid. Answer: está (location).
  3. Nosotros (somos / estamos) estudiantes. Answer: somos (identity).

All the forms of both verbs are in the ser and estar tables.

Questions

Is there a rule that always works?

No. "What it is" versus "where or how it is" works most of the time, but some phrases must simply be learned.

Why is the party es en mi casa?

Because events take ser even when a place follows. A thing located in a place takes estar.
