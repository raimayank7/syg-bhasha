bailar: conjugation

to dance

bailar means to dance. It is an -ar verb, and it is a regular verb: its endings follow the pattern of all verbs that end in -ar. The gerund is bailando and the past participle is bailado.

To conjugate it, remove -ar to get the stem bail and add the ending for the tense and person; in the future and conditional the whole infinitive is the base. The forms below were checked against Wiktionary.

The forms to learn first

If you know bailar (infinitive), bailando (gerund), bailado (past participle), bailo (I, present) and bailé (I, preterite), you can build most of the other forms, and you will recognise the verb when you meet it in a text.

The pattern it follows

All forms of bailar come from the stem bail plus the endings of regular -ar verbs, with the whole infinitive as the base in the future and the conditional. Present endings: -o, -as, -a, -amos, -áis, -an. Preterite endings: -é, -aste, -ó, -amos, -asteis, -aron.

Example sentences

  • Los niños bailan en el patio. In the present (ellos / ustedes, bailan). The children dance in the yard.
  • Nosotros bailamos toda la noche. In the preterite (nosotros, bailamos). We danced all night.

Using it with vosotros and ustedes

The table has a vosotros form (bailáis in the present), used in Spain for "you all" among friends. In Latin America people use ustedes with the third-person plural instead: bailan. Both are correct; choose the one your readers or teachers use.

Questions

What is the gerund of bailar?

bailando

What is the past participle of bailar?

bailado

Is bailar regular or irregular?

Regular: it follows the ordinary -ar pattern.

Related verbs

Other regular -ar verbs: hablar, trabajar, estudiar, comprar, caminar, cantar, cocinar, escuchar.
