Gurmukhi: letters, names and quiz

52 symbols of the Gurmukhi script (ਗੁਰਮੁਖੀ), used for Punjabi (in India) and the Sikh scriptures.

Gurmukhi is the script in which Punjabi is written in India. It is also the script of the Sikh scriptures, and its name is traditionally explained as "from the mouth of the Guru". It is written from left to right. It is an abugida: Each consonant letter carries a built-in vowel (usually "a"). Other vowels are shown by signs written around the consonant, and a special sign (the virama or its equivalent) removes the vowel so that two consonants can join.

Gurmukhi has three letters that carry the independent vowels, ੳ ਅ ੲ, and the vowels are made from them. Its first letters, ੳ ਅ ੲ ਸ ਹ, begin the traditional order of the alphabet, which is known as the paintee. The chart below follows the Unicode order, which starts with the vowel letters.

How this chart was made: the letters and their names are read from the Unicode Character Database (version 16.0.0), so they are not typed by hand. For this script the label is the usual Latin spelling of the letter's sound as it appears in its Unicode name. The sound labels are approximate and no native reader has checked this page.

How to learn it

  1. Learn the three vowel carriers and the vowel signs on the chart.
  2. Practise the letters in the traditional order, five at a time.
  3. Remember that Punjabi has tones that the letters ਘ ਝ ਢ ਧ ਭ help to mark.

Questions

Is Gurmukhi like Devanagari?

It is related to it: the sounds are arranged in a similar order, but the shapes are different and Gurmukhi has no line along the top of some letters.

What is the paintee?

It is the traditional order of the 35 letters, taught to children in Punjab.
