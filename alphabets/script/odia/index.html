Odia: letters, names and quiz

55 symbols of the Odia script (ଓଡ଼ିଆ), used for Odia (Oriya).

Odia is the script of the Odia language of Odisha. Its letters are round, with a curved top, because the script was traditionally written on palm leaves. It is written from left to right. It is an abugida: Each consonant letter carries a built-in vowel (usually "a"). Other vowels are shown by signs written around the consonant, and a special sign (the virama or its equivalent) removes the vowel so that two consonants can join.

The consonants follow the same order as the other Indian scripts of Sanskrit descent. The chart below shows the vowel letters, the consonants and the vowel signs.

How this chart was made: the letters and their names are read from the Unicode Character Database (version 16.0.0), so they are not typed by hand. For this script the label is the usual Latin spelling of the letter's sound as it appears in its Unicode name. The sound labels are approximate and no native reader has checked this page.

How to learn it

  1. Learn the vowel letters first.
  2. Practise the curved shapes slowly: many letters differ in one small stroke.
  3. Learn each vowel sign with କ.

Questions

Why are Odia letters round?

It is often explained by the palm leaves on which the script was written, where straight lines could split the leaf.
