Bengali: letters, names and quiz

53 symbols of the Bengali script (বাংলা), used for Bengali (Bangla) and Assamese.

Bengali is the script of Bangla, the language of Bangladesh and West Bengal, and with two more letters it writes Assamese. It is written from left to right with a line along the top. It is an abugida: Each consonant letter carries a built-in vowel (usually "a"). Other vowels are shown by signs written around the consonant, and a special sign (the virama or its equivalent) removes the vowel so that two consonants can join.

Several Bengali letters sound different from their Hindi cousins: the built-in vowel is closer to "o" than "a", and the letter ব is used for both b and v. The chart below gives the Unicode names of the letters, which use the Sanskrit-based names.

How this chart was made: the letters and their names are read from the Unicode Character Database (version 16.0.0), so they are not typed by hand. For this script the label is the usual Latin spelling of the letter's sound as it appears in its Unicode name. The sound labels are approximate and no native reader has checked this page.

How to learn it

  1. Learn the vowel letters and signs first.
  2. Notice letters that look alike: ব and র, ঘ and ঝ.
  3. Listen to a speaker for the vowel sounds, because they differ from Hindi.

Questions

Why does Bengali have two similar letters for "ja"?

The letters য and জ come from the Sanskrit alphabet, and in Bengali they are pronounced alike in most words.
