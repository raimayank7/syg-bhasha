Hiragana: letters, names and quiz

46 symbols of the Hiragana script (ひらがな), used for Japanese.

Hiragana is one of the two Japanese syllable scripts. Each symbol stands for a syllable (ka, ki, ku). It is written from left to right (or top to bottom) and is used for Japanese words, endings and particles. The chart shows the 46 basic symbols.

The Unicode name of each symbol gives its sound. The sounds are written in the traditional gojuon order, in rows of five: a i u e o, ka ki ku ke ko, and so on. Voiced and small forms are made with marks and are not in the chart.

How this chart was made: the letters and their names are read from the Unicode Character Database (version 16.0.0), so they are not typed by hand. For this script the label is the syllable as given in its Unicode name. The sound labels are approximate and no native reader has checked this page.

How to learn it

  1. Learn one row a day.
  2. Practise reading words: さくら (sakura).
  3. Learn katakana after hiragana: the sounds are the same.

Questions

How many hiragana are there?

There are 46 basic symbols; marks and small symbols add more sounds.
