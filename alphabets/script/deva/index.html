Devanagari: letters, names and quiz

55 symbols of the Devanagari script (देवनागरी), used for Hindi, Marathi, Nepali, Sanskrit and many other languages.

Devanagari is the script of Hindi, Marathi, Nepali and Sanskrit. It is written from left to right and hangs its letters from a horizontal line, the shirorekha. It is an abugida: Each consonant letter carries a built-in vowel (usually "a"). Other vowels are shown by signs written around the consonant, and a special sign (the virama or its equivalent) removes the vowel so that two consonants can join.

The letters are arranged by where the sound is made in the mouth. The consonants run from the throat (क ख ग घ ङ) to the palate, the tongue-tip, the teeth and the lips (प फ ब भ म), and then the half-vowels and the hissing sounds. Once you learn this order, the chart below reads like a map of the mouth.

How this chart was made: the letters and their names are read from the Unicode Character Database (version 16.0.0), so they are not typed by hand. For this script the label is the usual Latin spelling of the letter's sound as it appears in its Unicode name. The sound labels are approximate and no native reader has checked this page.

How to learn it

  1. Learn the 11 vowel letters first, then the five rows of five consonants in order.
  2. Learn the vowel signs together with one consonant, for example क का कि की कु कू, before you meet other consonants.
  3. Write every letter by hand: the shirorekha is drawn last, after the whole word.

Questions

Is Devanagari hard to learn?

The letters are regular, and each stands for one sound. Most learners can read slowly after a week of daily practice; conjunct letters take longer.

Which languages use it?

Hindi, Marathi, Nepali, Sanskrit and Konkani, among others.
