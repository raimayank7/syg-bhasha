Learn a new script in a week

Learning to read a new script is a small, finite job: you only have to learn between 24 and about 100 symbols, and the sounds are written regularly. Ten to twenty minutes a day for a week is enough to read slowly. This plan works for any script on this site.

Before you start

Choose one script. Open its page and print the chart, using the Print button of your browser. Find a short list of words you can already say that are written in the script: city names, food words, your own name.

The week

  1. Day 1: the vowels. Learn the vowel letters. Say each aloud, and write it three times.
  2. Day 2: the first row of consonants. Learn five to eight consonants. In an abugida, say each consonant with its built-in vowel.
  3. Day 3: more consonants. Another five to eight. Take the quiz on the page at the end of the session.
  4. Day 4: the rest of the consonants. Take the quiz again. Look again at the letters you got wrong.
  5. Day 5: vowel signs or special forms. For abugidas, learn the vowel signs with one consonant. For Greek and Cyrillic, learn the letters that look like Latin ones but sound different.
  6. Day 6: read real words. Read signs, menus, a news headline, the names of your own family. Sound them out slowly.
  7. Day 7: review and write. Write your name and five words you like. Take the quiz three times.

Using the quiz

The quiz shows a letter and four possible names or sounds. Pick one; the right answer is shown. It repeats at random, and you can leave at any time. It keeps score for the visit only and does not store anything.

Tips that help

  • Write by hand. Your hand remembers shapes that your eyes forget.
  • Learn letters in small groups, and test yourself on old groups before you add new ones.
  • Use a script for something real from day one: label things in your house, write a shopping list.
  • Find a speaker to listen to; a chart cannot teach every sound.

Questions

Do I need to learn the stroke order?

It helps with handwriting and with speed. This site does not show stroke order, because we have no source we can check it against; use a textbook or a teacher.

Can I learn two scripts at once?

It is better to finish one first. If the two are related, such as Hindi and Gujarati, the second will go faster.
