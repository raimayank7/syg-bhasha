Teaching the alphabet at home

A child of three or four does not need lessons. They need to meet letters often, in a happy way, with an adult who is in no hurry. This plan takes ten minutes a day. It works for the Hindi varnmala and for the Punjabi alphabet; where the two differ, the page says so.

Stage 1: hearing sounds (age 3 onwards)

Before any letter, play with first sounds. "What does मम्मी start with? Mmm." "Find something in this room that starts with प." This costs nothing and can be done in the kitchen or on a walk. A child who can hear the first sound of a word is ready for picture words.

Stage 2: reciting, with a finger on the chart

Put a chart at the child's eye level. Recite one row a day, pointing at each letter: क, ख, ग, घ, ङ. Reciting is singing, not reading, and children love it. The pointing finger is what slowly ties the sound to the shape. For Punjabi, recite the names: ਊੜਾ, ਐੜਾ, ਈੜੀ, ਸੱਸਾ, ਹਾਹਾ.

Stage 3: knowing letters out of order

This is the real test, and the step most often skipped. Choose three letters. Write each on a slip of paper. Mix them and ask, "Which one is म?" Add one new letter every day or two and take out the ones that are known for sure. The letter game on this site does the same thing on a screen.

Begin with letters that look very different from each other, such as म, ट, र, क. Keep look-alike pairs apart for the first weeks: घ and ध, भ and म, ब and व.

Stage 4: picture words and rhymes

Each letter page here gives a picture word and a one-line rhyme, like ग से गमला, फूल खिले. Say the rhyme, find the real thing if it is in the house, and let the child draw it. One letter a day is plenty. If the school chart uses another picture word, use the school's.

Stage 5: writing (usually after age 4 and a half)

  1. Lines first. Standing lines, sleeping lines, slanting lines, circles and waves, big, with crayons.
  2. Finger tracing. In sand, in flour on a plate, or on the tracing pad.
  3. Pencil tracing. Use the printed sheets, six letters to a page.
  4. Copying. The letter is shown and the child writes it beside the model.
  5. From memory. You say the sound; the child writes the letter.

Teach writing in the order of easy shapes, not in the order of the alphabet. In Hindi, र, ग, म, न, त, व, ब come early; ऋ, छ, क्ष, ज्ञ come last. The page on stroke order explains the families of shapes.

How long, how often

Ten minutes, once a day, at a fixed time, is better than an hour on Sunday. Stop before the child wants to. Always end with a letter the child knows well, so that the sitting finishes with a success.

Two languages at once?

Many families want both Hindi and Punjabi, and English besides. Children handle this well if the scripts are kept apart in time: one script in a sitting, never two charts side by side in the first months. Start the second script when the first is steady. Then the likenesses help, and the page Hindi and Punjabi letters side by side becomes useful.

What to skip

  • Do not teach the matras (vowel signs) until the letters are known out of order.
  • Do not drill the five letters that begin no word (ङ ञ ण ड़ ढ़). Recognising them is enough.
  • Do not compare the child with a cousin or classmate. The range of normal is wide: some children know all letters at four, others at six, and both read equally well at eight.

When to ask for help

If a child of six and a half still cannot remember letters taught many times, or avoids looking at print, mention it to the class teacher. It may be nothing, or the child may need a different way of teaching. This site gives general guidance and cannot judge a single child.
