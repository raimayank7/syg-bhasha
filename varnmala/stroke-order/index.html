How to write the letters: stroke order

There is no single official stroke order for Hindi or Punjabi letters, and handwriting books of different publishers differ in small ways. But the broad habits are shared, and a child who learns them writes faster and more neatly. This page gives the rules that hold almost everywhere. Where your child's school book shows something else, follow the book.

Four rules for both scripts

  1. Top to bottom. A stroke that stands upright is drawn downwards.
  2. Left to right. A stroke that lies flat is drawn from left to right, and the left part of a letter is written before the right part.
  3. Big shape first, small marks last. Dots, small hooks and vowel signs are added after the body of the letter.
  4. Lift the pencil as little as possible. Curves that flow into each other are written in one move.

Hindi: the top line comes last

Every Hindi letter hangs from a line on top, called the शिरोरेखा. Children are taught to write the letter first and draw this line at the end, from left to right. When a whole word is written, each letter is written without its line, and then one long line is drawn over the whole word. This is why Hindi words look like washing on a clothes line.

A few letters break the line: ध and भ start with a small curl where the line would be, and थ has a loop there. Leave that part of the line open.

Hindi: three families of shape

1. Letters with a tall stick on the right. This is the biggest family: ख ग घ च ज त थ ध न प ब भ म य ल व श ष स. Write the body on the left, then the stick from top to bottom, then the top line.

2. Letters with the stick in the middle. Only क and फ. Write the left part, the stick, the arm on the right, then the top line.

3. Letters with no tall stick. ट ठ ड ढ द र ह छ ङ and the vowels इ ई उ ऊ. They hang from the line by a short neck. Write the neck and the round body in one flowing move, then the line.

Knowing the family is half the work. When a child meets a new letter, ask first: where is the stick?

Punjabi: the top line usually comes first

Gurmukhi letters also hang from a top line, but most Punjabi teachers write the line first, left to right, and then draw the body down from it. The pencil often does not lift between the line and the body. Several letters have an open top, where the line covers only part of the letter or is missing: look closely at ੳ, ਅ, ਮ and ਗ on the Punjabi letters page and copy the gap exactly.

Gurmukhi has no family of tall sticks. Its letters are squarer, and many are built from the same few parts: a line, a loop and a hook. Compare ਪ, ਧ and ਖ, or ਸ and ਮ. Teaching look-alike letters on different days prevents mix-ups.

Common faults and their cures

  • Letters written from the bottom up. Draw a small green dot where the stroke begins. Children follow dots happily.
  • The top line drawn in pieces. In Hindi, ask for the whole word first and one line at the end.
  • Letters of different heights. Use paper with four lines, or the printed tracing sheets, where every model letter is the same size.
  • Mirror writing. Reversed letters are normal up to about six years of age. Correct gently, show the model again, and do not make it a worry.
  • A tight grip. A short, thick pencil or a crayon is easier for small hands than a long thin one.

Practise in three steps

First in the air and on a table top with a finger, big and slow. Then on the screen with the tracing pad. Then with a pencil on paper. Each Hindi letter page says which family the letter belongs to and describes its shape in words.
