Punjabi letters ੳ ਅ ੲ ਸ ਹ

Row 1 of the Gurmukhi alphabet: the name, sound and words of each letter, with the matching Hindi letter.

The first row of the Gurmukhi alphabet is unlike any row of the Hindi alphabet. Its first three letters, ੳ, ਅ and ੲ, are vowel carriers: they have no fixed sound of their own until a vowel sign is added (only ਅ can stand bare). The row ends with ਸ and ਹ. Because of its first three letters the whole alphabet is sometimes called "ੳ ਅ ੲ", the way English speakers say "ABC".

The letters of this row are called ਊੜਾ (oora), ਐੜਾ (aira), ਈੜੀ (eeri), ਸੱਸਾ (sassa), ਹਾਹਾ (haha). Children recite the names in this order, in a sing-song rhythm, long before they can write the shapes.

ੳ ਊੜਾ (oora)

ੳ is never written alone. It carries three vowels: ਉ (short u), ਊ (long oo) and ਓ (o). The open top of ਓ is a special shape used only with this letter.

Hindi letter with the same job: उ, ऊ, ओ.

Words

  • ਊਠ camel
  • ਉੱਲੂ owl
  • ਉਂਗਲ finger
  • ਉੱਨ wool

Trace ੳ

ਅ ਐੜਾ (aira)

ਅ alone is the short "a". With signs it gives ਆ (aa), ਐ (ai) and ਔ (au). It looks very like Hindi अ, which helps children who know one script to start the other.

Hindi letter with the same job: अ, आ, ऐ, औ.

Words that begin with it

  • ਅੰਬ mango
  • ਅਨਾਰ pomegranate
  • ਅੱਖ eye
  • ਅੱਗ fire

Trace ਅ

ੲ ਈੜੀ (eeri)

ੲ is never written alone. It carries ਇ (short i), ਈ (long ee) and ਏ (e).

Hindi letter with the same job: इ, ई, ए.

Words

  • ਇੱਟ brick
  • ਇਮਲੀ tamarind
  • ਇੱਕ one
  • ਈਦ Eid

Trace ੲ

ਸ ਸੱਸਾ (sassa)

The plain "s" of "sun". With a dot below it becomes ਸ਼, the "sh" sound.

Hindi letter with the same job: स.

Words that begin with it

  • ਸੇਬ apple
  • ਸੱਪ snake
  • ਸੂਰਜ sun
  • ਸੜਕ road

Trace ਸ

ਹ ਹਾਹਾ (haha)

The "h" of "hat" at the start of a word. In the middle or at the end of a word it is often not said as a full "h"; it changes the tone of the word instead.

Hindi letter with the same job: ह.

Words that begin with it

  • ਹਾਥੀ elephant
  • ਹੱਥ hand
  • ਹਿਰਨ deer
  • ਹਲ plough

Trace ਹ

Writing this row

In Gurmukhi the line on top is usually written first, from left to right, and the body of the letter is then hung from it. This is the opposite of the habit taught for Hindi, where the top line comes last. A few letters have an open top where the line is broken or missing; copy that gap exactly, because it is part of the letter. More on how the letters are written.
