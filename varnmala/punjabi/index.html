The painti: thirty-five letters

The Punjabi alphabet is called ਪੈਂਤੀ (painti), which simply means "thirty-five", after the number of its original letters. They stand in seven rows of five. The script is called Gurmukhi. It was given its present standard form in the sixteenth century by Guru Angad Dev Ji, the second Sikh Guru, and it is the script of the Guru Granth Sahib and the official script of Punjabi in India.

Every letter has a name

Unlike Hindi, where a letter is named by its sound, each Gurmukhi letter has a name of its own. ਕ is ਕੱਕਾ (kakka), ਮ is ਮੰਮਾ (mamma). Most names double the sound of the letter, so they are easy to learn. Three do not follow the pattern: ਊੜਾ, ਐੜਾ, ਈੜੀ for the three vowel carriers, and ਟੈਂਕਾ for ਟ.

What is different from Hindi

  • Three vowel carriers. Hindi has a separate letter for every vowel. Punjabi has three base letters, ੳ ਅ ੲ, which take signs to make all ten vowel sounds.
  • ਸ and ਹ come first. They stand in the first row, not at the end of the alphabet.
  • Tone letters. ਘ ਝ ਢ ਧ ਭ are written like the Hindi breathy letters but are said differently: they give the word a low or high tone.
  • No joined-letter rows. There are no letters like क्ष or ज्ञ. Punjabi uses very few joined forms, which makes reading simpler for a beginner.
  • Six dotted letters. ਸ਼ ਖ਼ ਗ਼ ਜ਼ ਫ਼ ਲ਼ were added later with a dot at the foot.

The rows

  • ੳ ਅ ੲ ਸ ਹ row 1
  • ਕ ਖ ਗ ਘ ਙ row 2
  • ਚ ਛ ਜ ਝ ਞ row 3
  • ਟ ਠ ਡ ਢ ਣ row 4
  • ਤ ਥ ਦ ਧ ਨ row 5
  • ਪ ਫ ਬ ਭ ਮ row 6
  • ਯ ਰ ਲ ਵ ੜ row 7
  • ਸ਼ ਖ਼ ਗ਼ ਜ਼ ਫ਼ ਲ਼ row 8
  • Vowel signs the ten lagan matra, bindi, tippi and addhak

If you already read Hindi, start with Hindi and Punjabi letters side by side: many shapes are close cousins, and a few look alike but mean different sounds.
