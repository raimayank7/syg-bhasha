How the chart is arranged

The Hindi alphabet begins with the vowels, स्वर, the sounds that can be said on their own with an open mouth. Then come the consonants, व्यंजन, which need a vowel to be spoken. The consonants are not in a random order. The first twenty-five stand in five rows of five, each row made at one place in the mouth, from the throat (क ख ग घ ङ) to the lips (प फ ब भ म). After them come य र ल व and श ष स ह. School charts end with the joined letters क्ष त्र ज्ञ श्र and often add ड़ and ढ़.

About the picture words

Every chart pairs a letter with a word that starts with it: अ से अनार, आ से आम. There is no official list, and the word differs from publisher to publisher: क may come with कबूतर or कमल. What matters is that the child hears the first sound of the word and ties it to the shape. Five letters, ङ ञ ण ड़ ढ़, never start a Hindi word, so they have no picture word; their pages show words that carry them inside.

A good order for learning

Recite the chart in order, but teach writing in the order of easy shapes. Many teachers begin with र, ग, म, न, व and ब, and leave ऋ, छ, क्ष and ज्ञ for the end. The guide teaching the alphabet at home explains a routine, and how many letters are there explains why different books give different totals.
