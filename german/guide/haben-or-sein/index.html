German Verbs

Haben or sein in the German perfect tense

In everyday German, past events are mostly told with the perfect tense (Perfekt), not with the simple past. It is made with a form of haben or sein and the past participle at the end of the sentence: "Ich habe Kaffee getrunken." (I drank / have drunk coffee.) "Wir sind nach Berlin gefahren." (We went to Berlin.)

Most verbs take haben

Use haben with most verbs, in particular with all verbs that take a direct object (kaufen, essen, lesen, machen) and with reflexive verbs: "Ich habe ein Buch gelesen."

Sein with movement and change

Use sein with verbs that show movement from one place to another (gehen, kommen, fahren, laufen, reisen) and verbs that show a change of state (werden, einschlafen, aufwachen), and with sein and bleiben themselves: "Er ist nach Hause gegangen." "Sie ist müde geworden." "Wir sind zu Hause geblieben."

Regional note

With verbs of position such as stehen, sitzen and liegen, Southern Germany, Austria and Switzerland use sein ("Ich bin gestanden"), while Northern Germany uses haben ("Ich habe gestanden"). The verb tables here show haben for stehen, which is the standard form taught in most courses. Some movement verbs such as fahren and schwimmen take haben when they have an object ("Er hat das Auto gefahren") and sein when they do not.

The participle

Weak (regular) verbs form the participle with ge- + stem + -t (gemacht, gespielt). Strong verbs change the stem and end in -en (gegangen, getrunken). The participle of each verb on this site is in its table, and each page also shows which auxiliary the verb takes.

Sentence position

The auxiliary is in second position and the participle goes to the end: "Gestern habe ich den ganzen Tag gearbeitet."

Questions

Do I use the perfect or the simple past when I speak?

In everyday German the perfect is the normal tense for talking about the past. The simple past (Präteritum) is common in writing and with sein, haben and the modal verbs.

Which verbs always take sein?

The verb sein itself, bleiben, werden, and verbs of movement from place to place or change of state without an object.
