The story of Hangul: who made it, when and why

Most writing systems grew slowly over centuries. Hangul, the Korean alphabet, was designed on purpose by a king and his scholars in the 15th century.

Before Hangul

Korea wrote with Chinese characters (hanja), which only educated people could learn. Ordinary people could not easily read or write. Korean sounds did not fit well into Chinese characters.

King Sejong

King Sejong the Great (reigned 1418 to 1450) is credited with creating the alphabet with a group of scholars. It was completed in 1443 and announced in 1446 with a document called Hunminjeongeum, "The correct sounds for the instruction of the people." The new script was meant to be easy enough for anyone to learn.

How the letters were designed

  • The basic consonants are said to imitate the shape of the mouth or tongue when you make the sound: ㄱ for a sound made at the back of the mouth, ㄴ for the tongue touching the upper teeth, ㅁ for the shape of the lips, ㅅ for the teeth, ㅇ for the throat.
  • More letters are built by adding strokes, which shows how related sounds belong together (ㄱ, ㅋ, ㄲ).
  • The vowels are based on a vertical line (a person), a horizontal line (the earth) and a dot (the sky): ㅣ, ㅡ and ㆍ. The dot later became a short stroke in ㅏ, ㅓ, ㅗ, ㅜ.

Syllable blocks

Hangul letters are written in square blocks, one block per syllable: ㅎ + ㅏ + ㄴ = 한. This is why Korean text looks like a series of small squares. The syllable builder shows how it works.

How many letters

Modern Hangul has 14 basic consonants and 10 basic vowels (24 letters), plus 5 doubled consonants and 11 combined vowels, so 40 in all. These are the letters on the consonants and vowels pages.

Hangul Day

South Korea marks the creation of the alphabet on 9 October (Hangeul Day, 한글날), which is a public holiday. North Korea marks it on a different date.

Questions

Who invented Hangul?

King Sejong the Great and scholars at his court, in the 1440s. It was announced in 1446.

Is Hangul hard to learn?

Many learners can read it in a few days, because each letter is a simple shape and the rules are regular, but pronunciation rules take longer.
