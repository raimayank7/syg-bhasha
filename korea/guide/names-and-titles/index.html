Korean names and titles: how to address people

Koreans use titles much more than English speakers, and avoid calling older people by their given names. This guide gives the basics.

Order of a name

The family name comes first and the given name second: 김민수 is the family name 김 (Kim) and the given name 민수 (Minsu). Most Korean names have three syllables: one for the family name and two for the given name. Many family names have just one syllable, and Kim, Lee and Park are among the commonest.

Adding a respectful word

  • -씨 after the full name or the given name is polite and neutral: 민수 씨. Do not use -씨 for someone clearly senior to you.
  • -님 is more respectful and used with a title or a profession: 선생님 (teacher), 사장님 (company president), 고객님 (customer).
  • Many people are called by their job title at work, with -님 added: 김 부장님 (Department Head Kim).

Family titles used with friends too

Korean has separate words for older siblings, and they are used with friends and with people who are not related:

WordUsed byMeaning
형 (hyeong)a manolder brother, or an older male friend
누나 (nuna)a manolder sister, or an older female friend
오빠 (oppa)a womanolder brother, or an older male friend
언니 (eonni)a womanolder sister, or an older female friend

Do not use "you"

The word for "you" (당신) is rarely used in speech and can sound rude or intimate. Koreans repeat the person's name and title instead, or leave the subject out.

Introducing yourself

A common line is 저는 [name]입니다 (I am [name]), followed by 만나서 반갑습니다 (nice to meet you).

A note on this page

No native speaker has read this page. Customs differ between families, regions and workplaces.

Questions

Should I use first names with Koreans?

Not with elders or at work. Use the full name or a title with -씨 or -님, and let the other person suggest something more casual.

Do married women change their family name?

Traditionally Korean women keep their family name after marriage.
