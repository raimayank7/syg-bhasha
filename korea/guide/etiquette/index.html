Korean etiquette: greetings, gifts, meals and home visits

Customs vary between people, but these points are widely described and help visitors avoid mistakes.

Greeting

  • A slight bow is the usual greeting, often with a handshake in business. Bow a little deeper to someone older or senior.
  • When giving or receiving something, many people use both hands, or the right hand supported by the left at the wrist or forearm, as a sign of respect.

Visiting a home

  • Take off your shoes at the door. Houses have a slightly lower entrance area for shoes.
  • Bring a small gift such as fruit or something sweet, and offer it with both hands.
  • Greet the oldest person first.

Eating

  • Wait for the oldest person to start before you begin eating.
  • Use a spoon for rice and soup and chopsticks for side dishes. Do not stick chopsticks upright in a bowl of rice, which resembles incense offered to the dead.
  • Pour drinks for others with two hands, and receive a drink with two hands. Do not pour your own drink at a formal meal; someone else will do it.
  • Share the dishes in the middle of the table. It is polite to finish what is on your plate.

Paying and tipping

Tipping is not usual in restaurants in South Korea. At a group meal, one person often pays for everyone, and it is polite to offer to pay the next time.

Public behaviour

  • Priority seats on trains and buses are for the elderly, pregnant women and people with disabilities. Do not sit in them if someone who needs them is nearby.
  • Speak quietly on public transport and take calls briefly.

A note on this page

These are general customs for visitors in South Korea, written by us from widely published guidance, and no Korean person has reviewed them. Follow the lead of your hosts.

Questions

Do I tip in Korea?

Tipping is not customary in South Korea. A kind word or a thank-you is enough.

Why use two hands?

Using both hands when giving or receiving is a sign of respect, especially towards elders and seniors.
