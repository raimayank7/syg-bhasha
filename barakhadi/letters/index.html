How the letters are arranged

The Hindi alphabet is not in a random order. The first twenty-five consonants stand in five rows of five, and each row is made at one place in the mouth, moving from the throat forward to the lips: क ख ग घ ङ at the back of the mouth, च छ ज झ ञ on the palate, ट ठ ड ढ ण with the tongue curled back, त थ द ध न on the teeth and प फ ब भ म with the lips. Inside every row the order is also fixed: plain, breathy, voiced, voiced and breathy, nasal. Once a child hears this pattern in one row, the other four rows come quickly.

After the five rows come the four in-between letters य र ल व, the four hissing and breathing letters श ष स ह, and the joined letters क्ष त्र ज्ञ that schools teach with the alphabet.

Which rows to learn first

There is no need to follow the alphabet. Start with letters that make many easy words: क, म, न, र, ल, प and ब. Leave ङ, ञ and ण for the end: no word begins with them, and their pages say so plainly. The letter र deserves a day of its own because of रु and रू.

What is on each letter page

  • The row in large letters, with a button to hear it
  • How the sound is made in the mouth and which letters it is confused with
  • The twelve forms as cards, each with its English spelling and an example word where one exists
  • Two short sentences to read
  • Grey letters to trace, and a link to a printable practice sheet

To see everything on one page, open the full chart. To study one sign across all letters, use the matra pages.
