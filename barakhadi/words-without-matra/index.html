Barakhadi

Words without matra (बिना मात्रा के शब्द)

Before the barakhadi, a child should taste real reading. Words with no matra at all are perfect for this: every letter is read just as it was learnt in the alphabet. The lists below are graded by length. Each word has its meaning, so that reading and understanding grow together.

How to use these lists

  1. Start with the two-letter words. Point to each letter, say the two sounds, then say them together: घ, र, घर.
  2. Do not say the hidden अ at the end. घर is "ghar", not "ghara".
  3. Read five new words a day and re-read yesterday's five.
  4. Ask what the word means, or ask the child to use it in a spoken sentence.
  5. Move to the next list when a list is read at talking speed.

Short sentences with no matra

Once single words are easy, join them. These lines use only matra-free words, so they sound a little like commands, but a child can read every one alone:

  • घर चल। (Come home.)
  • नल बंद कर। (Turn off the tap. बंद has a bindi, the first sign most children learn.)
  • जल भर। (Fill the water.)
  • फल रख। (Keep the fruit.)
  • अब मत डर। (Do not be afraid now.)
  • झटपट चल। (Walk quickly.)
  • कलम पकड़। (Hold the pen.)
  • सड़क पर मत चल। (Do not walk on the road.)

What comes next

When the four-letter list is comfortable, begin the first matra: आ की मात्रा. From there, take one matra a week in chart order. The plan on how to teach barakhadi at home lays out the whole path.
