Common barakhadi mistakes

Nearly every learner of Hindi makes the same handful of mistakes. They are not signs of carelessness. They come from places where the script itself is tricky. Knowing them in advance saves weeks.

1. Short and long vowels

ि and ी, ु and ू are pairs. The short one is said in one beat, the long one in two. In writing, the short इ stands before the letter and the long ई after it; the short उ curls back and the long ऊ swings forward. Pairs of real words fix the difference better than rules:

  • दिन (day) and दीन (poor)
  • मिल (meet, mill) and मील (mile)
  • कुल (total) and कूल (bank of a river)
  • सुना (heard) and सूना (deserted)

2. रु and रू

For every letter but one, ु and ू hang below. For र they sit on the right side. रु has a small hook: रुपया, गुरु. रू has a longer curl: रूप, शुरू. Many adults write these two wrongly all their lives. Practise them on their own line.

3. Look-alike letters

Four pairs cause most reading errors:

  • घ and ध. घ has a full top line. ध has a curl and a broken top line.
  • भ and म. भ has a curl and a broken top line. म has a full line and a loop at the bottom.
  • ब and व. ब has a slanting line inside the belly. व is empty.
  • प and ष. ष has a slanting line inside. प is empty.

A matra on top does not change which letter it is. Teach the child to look at the body of the letter first and the matra second.

4. One stroke or two

के and कै, को and कौ differ by a single small stroke. In fast handwriting the second stroke is often dropped, and मैला turns into मेला. Ask the child to write the strokes clearly apart at first, even if it looks slow.

5. Bindi and chandrabindu

The plain dot (ं) is the anusvara: a nasal consonant sound, as in गंगा and बंदर. The half-moon with a dot (ँ) is the chandrabindu: the vowel itself is said through the nose, as in माँ, चाँद and आँख. When a matra already sits above the top line, there is no room for the half-moon, so only the dot is written: में, नहीं, हैं. This is a spelling convention; the sound is still nasal.

6. The extra "a" at the end

Each consonant has अ built in, but Hindi does not say it at the end of a word. राम is "Raam", not "Raama"; कमल is "kamal". Children who learn the chart by chanting often read every letter with its vowel. Reading whole words in sentences corrects this quickly.

7. Hard and soft t and d

ट ठ ड ढ are said with the tongue curled back against the roof of the mouth. त थ द ध are said with the tongue on the teeth. English has only one "t" and one "d", so learners who think in English mix them. Compare दाल (lentils) and डाल (branch), ताला (lock) and टाला (put off).

8. Breath or no breath

क and ख, ग and घ, प and फ, ब and भ differ only by a puff of air. Hold a hand before the mouth: for the second of each pair you should feel the breath. खाना (food) and काना (one-eyed) show why it matters.

9. Half letters read as full

In क्या, स्कूल and प्यार the first letter has lost its vowel and is joined to the next. Reading it as कया or सकूल is a common early slip. The page on half letters shows how these joins are built.

How to practise the fixes

Take one mistake a week. Write five pairs of words on a card, read them aloud daily, and use the quiz to check. The practice sheets can print any row you choose.
