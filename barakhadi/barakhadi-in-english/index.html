Barakhadi

Barakhadi in English letters

Parents who did not study in Hindi medium, and learners outside India, often want the barakhadi written in English letters. The chart below gives every form in a simple spelling that needs no special marks. Read the notes first, because English letters cannot show every Hindi sound exactly.

How the twelve endings are spelt here

  • a: short, as in "about" (क = ka)
  • aa: long, as in "father" (का = kaa)
  • i: short, as in "pin" (कि = ki)
  • ee: long, as in "feet" (की = kee)
  • u: short, as in "put" (कु = ku)
  • oo: long, as in "moon" (कू = koo)
  • e: as in "day", without the glide (के = ke)
  • ai: close to the "a" in "cat" (कै = kai)
  • o: as in "go", without the glide (को = ko)
  • au: close to "aw" in "saw" (कौ = kau)
  • an: the nasal dot; also written "am" or "ang" (कं = kan)
  • ah: the soft breath of the visarga (कः = kah)

Sounds that English letters blur

Hindi has two kinds of t and d. ट ठ ड ढ are hard, with the tongue curled back. त थ द ध are soft, with the tongue on the teeth. In plain English spelling both rows come out as t, th, d, dh. In the chart the hard row is marked with a capital letter (Ta, Tha, Da, Dha) so that you can tell them apart. This capital-letter habit is common in typed Hindi, but it is not an official standard.

The letter "h" after a consonant means breath, not a new sound: kh is a breathy k, and th is a breathy t, never the "th" of "think". च is written ch and छ is chh. श and ष are both sh. ज्ञ is spelt gya, the way it is spoken in Hindi.

Why spellings differ from book to book

There is an official scholarly system for writing Hindi in Roman letters, with dots and lines over letters (ā, ī, ṭ, ḍ). It is exact, but few people can type or read it. Everyday writing uses loose spellings, so आ may be a, aa or A, and ई may be i, ee or ii. Names show the same variety: "Deepak" and "Dipak" are one name. The spelling on this page was chosen because it is the one most parents type when they search.

Use the Roman chart as a crutch, not as a path

The English spelling is a help for the adult who is teaching, or for a learner who wants to check a sound. It should not replace the Devanagari chart. A child who learns to read "kaa" will still not be able to read का. Point to the Hindi form, say the sound, and glance at the English only when you are unsure. The sound itself is best learnt by ear: on the main chart you can tap any form to hear it, if your device has a Hindi voice.
