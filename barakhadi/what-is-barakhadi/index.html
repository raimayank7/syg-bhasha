Barakhadi

What is barakhadi?

Barakhadi (बारहखड़ी) is the chart that shows every Hindi consonant joined with the vowel signs, one after the other: क का कि की कु कू के कै को कौ कं कः. The name comes from बारह, twelve, because each consonant gets twelve forms. Children in Hindi-medium schools learn it right after the alphabet, and it is the bridge between knowing letters and reading words.

Varnmala and barakhadi are two different steps

The varnmala (वर्णमाला) is the alphabet: the vowels अ to औ and the consonants क to ह, each learnt as a separate letter. The barakhadi is what happens when a consonant and a vowel meet. In Hindi a vowel that follows a consonant is not written as a full letter. It becomes a small sign called a matra (मात्रा) that is attached to the consonant: above it, below it, before it or after it. क + आ is not written कआ. It is written का.

So a child who knows the varnmala can name letters, but cannot yet read कमला or किताब. The barakhadi teaches the ten matras and two extra signs, on every consonant, until reading them is automatic.

Why twelve?

The twelve forms follow the vowels in their alphabet order:

  1. अ: no sign, because every consonant already contains it (क)
  2. आ: a stick after the letter (का)
  3. इ: a flag before the letter (कि)
  4. ई: a flag after the letter (की)
  5. उ: a hook below (कु)
  6. ऊ: a tail below (कू)
  7. ए: one stroke above (के)
  8. ऐ: two strokes above (कै)
  9. ओ: a stick with one stroke (को)
  10. औ: a stick with two strokes (कौ)
  11. अं: a dot above, the anusvara (कं)
  12. अः: two dots after, the visarga (कः)

The last two are not true vowels. Grammar books call them ayogavaha. They are kept in the chart because they are written with the same kind of small sign and children meet them early, in words like बंदर and छः.

What about ऋ?

The vowel ऋ also has a matra (ृ), as in कृपा and गृह. Some printed charts add it and show thirteen forms. Most school charts leave it out, because it appears only in words from Sanskrit and is taught a little later. This site follows the common twelve-form chart and covers ृ on the page about other signs.

Which letters are in the chart?

The full chart on the home page has the 33 consonants from क to ह and the three joined letters क्ष, त्र and ज्ञ that schools teach with the alphabet. That makes 36 rows and 432 boxes. Not every box is a real building block of words: ङ and ञ never start a Hindi word, and forms such as यि or वू are almost never seen. They are in the chart for completeness and for practice in joining a sign to a shape.

How to use the chart

  • Read across. One row at a time, aloud, in the fixed rhythm: क का कि की, कु कू के कै, को कौ कं कः.
  • Read down. Take one matra and read it on every letter: का खा गा घा. This fixes the sign rather than the row. The matra pages are built for this.
  • Move to words quickly. A form is learnt when the child can read it inside a word. Each letter page gives a word for the forms that have one.

Is barakhadi only for Hindi?

No. Marathi, Nepali and other languages written in Devanagari use the same idea, and Marathi calls it बाराखडी. Gujarati, Punjabi and Bengali have their own scripts but build syllables in the same way. This site covers the Hindi chart as taught in Indian schools.
