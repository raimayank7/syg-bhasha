Barakhadi

How to teach barakhadi at home

Most children learn the barakhadi between the ages of four and seven. It does not need a tutor or a workbook. It needs ten quiet minutes a day, a pencil and an adult who does not hurry. This plan is the order that works for most children; change the pace to suit yours.

Before you start

Check that the child can recognise the consonants out of order. Point to त, म, घ on a page and ask for the names. If half of them are shaky, spend another two weeks on the alphabet first. Matras on top of unknown letters only confuse.

Step 1: words without any matra (one week)

Begin with reading, not with the chart. Two-letter words with no matra, such as घर, जल, फल and नल, let the child feel what reading is. Move to three and four letters: कमल, बरतन. The page of words without matra has graded lists. Stop this stage when the child reads a new word without spelling it out.

Step 2: one matra at a time (about a week each)

Teach the matras in chart order, and start each with the sign alone. Show how आ becomes a stick. Then:

  1. Read the sign on five easy letters: का, ना, मा, रा, ला.
  2. Read real words: काम, नाना, माला.
  3. Write the sign on a few letters, large, on plain paper.
  4. Read two short sentences.

Do not go to the next matra until this one is read without help. The long and short pairs (ि and ी, ु and ू) need the most patience. Teach them on different days, then put them side by side: दिन and दीन, बुरा and बूरा.

Step 3: the full row (two to three weeks)

Only now bring out the chart. Read one row a day aloud in rhythm, the adult first and the child after. The rhythm matters: four beats, four beats, four beats. Children pick it up like a song. After the reading, cover the row and ask for three forms out of order: "show me कू", "what is this one?".

Step 4: mixed reading

When the rows are known, practise words that mix matras: किताब, गुलाब, तोता, पौधा. Short sentences on each letter page of this site are written for this stage. A story book with large print can now take over.

How long each day?

Ten minutes is enough for a five-year-old, fifteen for a seven-year-old. End while it is still going well. Two short sittings beat one long one, and a missed day does no harm.

Writing practice

Reading comes before writing. A child can read का long before the hand can draw a straight stick. Use the practice sheets for tracing, and let early writing be big: a slate, a tray of sand or a finger on a steamed-up window all work.

Things that slow a child down

  • Chanting without looking. A child can sing the whole chart from memory and still not read कि. Always point at each form while saying it.
  • Roman spelling too early. Writing "ka kaa ki kee" under the chart helps adults. For a child who is still learning English letters it is a second code to break. Keep it for later.
  • Correcting every slip. Let small errors pass in the middle of a sentence. Come back to the word after the sentence is finished.
  • Testing in front of others. Performance for relatives turns reading into an exam.

Signs that it is working

The child starts reading shop boards, biscuit packets and bus signs without being asked. When that happens the barakhadi has done its job, and you can put the chart away.
