Barakhadi

Other signs beyond the twelve

The twelve forms of the barakhadi cover most of what a child reads in the first years. A few more signs turn up soon after. They are simple once you know what each one does.

The ऋ matra (ृ)

ऋ is the seventh vowel of the Hindi alphabet. Its sign is a small hook below the letter that opens to the right, like a tiny "c": कृ, गृ, मृ. In Hindi it is said "ri", so कृपा is "kripa" and ऋषि is "rishi". It is found only in words that came from Sanskrit:

  • कृपा: kindness, grace
  • गृह: house
  • मृग: deer
  • वृक्ष: tree
  • पृथ्वी: the earth
  • दृश्य: scene
  • हृदय: heart (ह and ऋ make the special shape हृ)

Do not confuse कृ with क्र. क्र is क joined with र (as in क्रम, order); कृ is क with the vowel ऋ. They sound close but the spelling is fixed for each word.

Chandrabindu (ँ)

The half-moon with a dot makes a vowel nasal: the air goes through the nose while the vowel is said. माँ, चाँद, आँख, पाँच, ऊँट, गाँव. Compare हंस (swan), which has a clear "n" sound, with हँस (laugh), which has only a nasal vowel. When a matra already stands above the top line, the half-moon is dropped and only the dot is written, as in मैं and नहीं.

Nukta (़)

A dot below a letter changes its sound. Two of these are needed for native Hindi words:

  • ड़: a quick flap of the tongue, as in लड़का, पेड़, सड़क
  • ढ़: the same flap with breath, as in पढ़ना, बूढ़ा, चढ़ना

These two never start a word. Five more are used for sounds from Urdu, Persian and English: क़ (क़लम), ख़ (ख़बर), ग़ (ग़लत), ज़ (ज़मीन, ज़ू) and फ़ (फ़ोन, फ़ीस). Of these, ज़ and फ़ are the ones you will see most. Many books and newspapers leave the dot out, and readers are expected to know the word.

Halant (्)

A small slanting stroke below a letter removes its built-in अ. क् is just "k". You rarely see the halant itself in print, because the bare consonant usually joins the next letter and becomes a half letter: क् + या = क्या. It is visible where a join is not possible or at the end of some Sanskrit words. See half letters and joined letters.

The half-moon ऑ (ॉ)

English words with the "o" of "doctor" or "ball" are written with a half-moon above the stick: डॉक्टर, कॉलेज, बॉल, ऑफ़िस. This sign has no dot. It is not the chandrabindu and it is not nasal. It was added to Hindi writing for borrowed words, so it is not part of the traditional chart.

Visarga and anusvara again

The two dots (ः) and the single dot (ं) are already in the chart as the eleventh and twelfth forms. Their pages are anusvara and visarga.

The danda

A Hindi sentence ends with an upright line (।) called the purn viram or danda, not with a full stop. Question marks, commas and exclamation marks are the same as in English.

What order to teach them in

After the twelve forms: first chandrabindu (it is in माँ, the first word many children write), then ड़ and ढ़, then half letters, then the ऋ matra, and last the nukta letters and ऑ, which the child will mostly meet in borrowed words.
