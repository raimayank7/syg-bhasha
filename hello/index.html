What you will find here

People look up "how to say thank you in Tamil" or "hello in Japanese" for many reasons: a new colleague, a trip, a neighbour, a wedding with guests from another region, a child learning a heritage language, or plain curiosity. This site puts the twelve phrases people need first in one place, for 26 languages, so you can compare them side by side or look at one language in depth.

The languages are chosen for readers in India and its neighbours: Hindi, Punjabi, Urdu, Bengali, Tamil, Telugu, Marathi, Gujarati, Kannada and Malayalam, then the languages of study, work and travel abroad: Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Russian, Japanese, Korean, Chinese, Arabic, Turkish, Indonesian, Thai, Vietnamese, Greek and Swahili.

How each phrase is shown

The phrase comes first in its own script, so you can show it to a native speaker or read it if you know the letters. Next to it, in italics, is a sound spelling in English letters; capitals mark the syllable to stress. The Listen button speaks the phrase with your device's voice where it has one, and Copy puts the phrase and its sound spelling on your clipboard to paste into a message. The guide to the sound spellings lists the rules we followed and what they cannot show.

Questions

Is this a full phrasebook?

No. It covers the twelve phrases that matter first and the numbers one to ten. For fuller learning, try a course with a teacher or the ShareYourGroup language sites on Urdu, Bangla, Korean and spoken English.

Can I trust the translations?

They are the standard everyday forms, written with care, and the numbers were cross-checked in 16 languages. But no native speaker has read the pages yet, so use them as a guide and correct us if you see a slip.
