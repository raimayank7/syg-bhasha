How to read the sound spellings on this site

Next to each phrase in its own writing, this site shows a sound spelling made of ordinary English letters. It is a bridge, not a science. It lets you say the words in a way that a speaker will probably understand, without learning a new alphabet first. This page explains the rules we followed and the things a sound spelling cannot do.

The rules we use

  • Capital letters mark the stressed syllable. In na-ma-STAY you press harder on STAY. A language with no strong stress, such as Japanese or Korean, still gets one capital group to show where the voice rises, but the stress there is much lighter.
  • Hyphens split syllables. Read each piece, then join them smoothly: sa-wat-DEE becomes "sawatdee".
  • Vowels are the plain vowels of English. We write "a" for the sound in "father" or "about", "e" for the sound in "bed" (or "ay" for the sound in "day"), "i" for the sound in "bit", "ee" for "see", "o" for "go", "oo" for "food", and "u" for "put". When a sound has no English equivalent, the nearest one is used.
  • Double letters mean a longer sound. In Hindi, "aa" is a long vowel, as in pyaar.
  • "kh", "gh" and "dh" are a single breathy sound in Indian languages (a "k" with a puff of air), not two sounds. The "kh" in Arabic, German and Russian is a throaty scrape, as in "loch".

What a sound spelling cannot show

Tones. Chinese, Thai and Vietnamese use pitch to tell words apart, and the same syllable with a different pitch is a different word. For Chinese we also print the pinyin with tone marks, but only a recording or a speaker teaches the tones.

Sounds English does not have. Hindi has several kinds of "t" and "d" where English has one each. The Tamil "zh" is a curled-back sound. Arabic has "ayn" and a deep "q". French has a rolled throat "r". These come only from listening, and a sound spelling gives the nearest letter.

Regional accents. Spanish in Spain says "gracias" with a "th", and in Latin America with an "s". Portuguese in Brazil and in Portugal differ in many vowels. Where the difference is large, we pick the most widely taught form.

How to practise

  1. Say the sound spelling slowly, syllable by syllable, with the stress.
  2. Use the Listen button on each phrase to hear your device's voice for that language, where it has one. Many phones and computers have no voice for languages such as Kannada, so the button will tell you.
  3. Repeat the phrase three times at normal speed.
  4. Say it to a speaker if you can, and copy how they say it.

If a speaker corrects you, trust the speaker over the sound spelling. We are always glad to hear about a mistake: please tell us.

Questions

Why not use a phonetic alphabet?

Because most people do not know it. Ordinary English letters are quicker to start with, and the native writing next to them is there for those who can read it.

Why does the same sound look different in two languages?

Each sound spelling is written to suit an English reader of that one language. The same letters can hint at slightly different sounds from language to language.
