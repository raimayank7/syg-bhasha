Say Hello

Learn greetings in a language in one week

You do not need a course to learn the twelve phrases on this site. Ten minutes a day for a week is enough to say hello, thank you, sorry and goodbye in a language you have never studied, well enough to be understood and to make a good first impression. Here is a plan that works with any of the 26 languages.

Before you start

Choose one language. Do not try two in the same week; the phrases blur. Open its language page on this site and keep it open. Decide who you will say the words to: a friend, a colleague, a neighbour, a speaker online or the mirror.

The seven days

  1. Day 1: hello and goodbye. Learn these two first. Say each ten times aloud, once with the stress as shown, once at normal speed. Use hello for the rest of the day, even to yourself.
  2. Day 2: thank you and please. These are the words you will use most. Add a gesture or a bow if the language has one (see the greeting customs guide).
  3. Day 3: sorry, yes and no. Learn the polite forms. Notice whether the language repeats a verb for yes (Chinese) or has a plain word.
  4. Day 4: good morning and good night. Say good morning when you wake and good night when you go to bed. A habit fixes them faster than a list.
  5. Day 5: how are you, and the answer. Learn the question and "fine, thank you". Practise a short exchange of four lines, playing both people.
  6. Day 6: welcome, I love you, and numbers one to ten. Numbers help with prices and phone numbers. Count objects around you aloud.
  7. Day 7: review. Cover the answer and test yourself on all twelve, then try a full conversation: hello, how are you, fine thank you, please, thank you, goodbye.

Tips that help

  • Say the words aloud. Reading silently does not teach the mouth.
  • Use the Listen button to hear the voice on your device, or find a speaker on a video to copy.
  • Learn the native writing of the first three words as well. Even a few characters help with signs and menus.
  • Do not worry about mistakes. Speakers nearly always appreciate the effort.

After the week

Add the days of the week, family words and food words, one set at a time. If you want to go further into a language, the ShareYourGroup language sites on Urdu, Bangla, Korean, Sanskrit and spoken English go deeper than a phrase list.

Questions

How many words can I really learn in a week?

Twelve phrases and ten numbers, about twenty-two items, is a realistic amount for ten minutes a day.

Will I be understood?

Mostly yes, if you stress the right syllable. In tonal languages (Chinese, Thai, Vietnamese) you may need to hear the tone from a speaker.
