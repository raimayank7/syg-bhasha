Yes looks like the simplest word to learn, but it has surprises. The table gives the common form in each language, and the notes below explain how yes behaves, because it does not always mean agreement.

Yes as "I hear you"

In Japanese, hai often means "I am listening" and not "I agree". A speaker may say hai many times in a conversation while still thinking. In Korea, ne is similar. In India, a side-to-side wobble of the head can mean yes, I understand, or okay, and it confuses many visitors. The Hindi haan (yes) and the polite ji haan are clearer. The Punjabi haan ji and Urdu ji haan add respect.

No single word for yes

Chinese has no simple word for yes. People repeat the verb: "Are you going?" is answered with "go" (qù), and the word shì shown in the table means "is" and works when the question is about being. Thai, Vietnamese and others do the same. Learners who say shì to every question will sometimes be wrong. Listen to how a speaker answers.

Yes in different registers

The Spanish sí and Italian sì carry an accent to show they are not the word "if". The French oui becomes ouais in casual talk. German ja is often shortened to jo in the south. The Russian da is also used for "I understand". The Greek ναι looks like the English "nai" but is said "neh", so it is a word to learn by ear.

Questions

Does a head wobble mean yes in India?

It can mean yes, okay, or I understand. If a decision matters, ask for a spoken answer.

What is the polite yes in Hindi?

"Ji haan" or "ji". Adding "ji" shows respect.
