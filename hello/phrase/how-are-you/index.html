"How are you?" is often a greeting and not a real question. In many languages the expected answer is "fine, thank you", whatever is the truth. Still, in a few cultures the question is meant, and a friend will expect a real answer. The table gives a polite form of the question in each language; friends use shorter, casual versions.

Literal meanings

The Hindi aap kaise hain and Urdu aap kaise hain mean "how are you (respectful)". The Gujarati tame kem chho and Marathi tumhi kase aahat are the same question. The Tamil eppadi irukkireergal asks "how are you being?". The Malayalam sukhamano asks "is it comfortable?". The Russian kak dela means "how are things", and the German wie geht es Ihnen means "how does it go with you". The Swahili habari yako means "your news". In each case the question is about your state, your affairs or your news.

What to answer

The usual reply is "I am fine, and you?" in the same language: main theek hoon, aap? in Hindi, bien, gracias, ¿y tú? in Spanish, genki desu, anata wa? in Japanese. In the Gulf and Levant, a long exchange of blessings follows the greeting before any business begins, and rushing it is felt to be abrupt. In Germany or Russia, the question is taken more literally, and a real answer can follow.

Polite and informal forms

The table uses the polite form where there is one. Between friends, Hindi speakers say kya haal hai, Spanish speakers ¿qué tal?, and French speakers ça va ?. A man and a woman may be asked with different endings in languages like Arabic, so do not be surprised if the other person says a slightly different word.

Questions

Should I answer honestly?

It depends on the language and the person. With acquaintances, a short positive answer is the custom nearly everywhere. With close friends, an honest answer is welcome.

What is the casual form in Hindi?

"Kaise ho?" to a friend of your age, "kya haal hai?" or "sab theek?" among peers. "Aap kaise hain?" is the respectful form for elders and strangers.
