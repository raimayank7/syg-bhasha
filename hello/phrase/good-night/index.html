Good night is said when someone is going to sleep, not when you meet them in the evening. For an evening hello many languages use a different phrase, such as the Spanish buenas tardes or the Indonesian selamat sore. The table gives the parting wish for bedtime in each language.

Wishes for sleep

The Russian spokoynoy nochi means "of a calm night", wishing quiet. The Japanese oyasumi nasai means "please rest", and friends and family shorten it to oyasumi. The Arabic tusbih ala khayr means "may you wake up well". The Korean annyeonghi jumuseyo asks the person to "sleep in peace". The Swahili lala salama says "sleep safely". A good night is, in every case, a kind wish for the hours ahead.

Hindi, Urdu and the regional languages

Hindi shubh ratri, Marathi shubh ratri and Gujarati shubh ratri are built from the same Sanskrit words meaning "auspicious night". Urdu uses shab bakhair, from Persian, with the same meaning. The Tamil iniya iravu means "sweet night". Many families in India also simply say "good night" in English, or "so jao" (go to sleep) in Hindi to children.

Good night as a goodbye

In English, good night can also mean goodbye at the end of an evening visit. In some languages it works the same way, for example the French bonne nuit and German gute Nacht, which are said when a guest leaves late. In others it does not: in Spanish, buenas noches is a greeting on arrival as well as a farewell. If you are unsure, check the time and the setting.

Questions

Is good night also used as hello in the evening?

In Spanish and Indonesian the same phrase works for both. In French, German, Hindi and most others, it is only for parting at bedtime; use a different evening hello.

What do parents say to children in India?

Many say "shubh ratri" or "so jao beta/beti" in Hindi, and the equivalent in their own language, often with a blessing.
