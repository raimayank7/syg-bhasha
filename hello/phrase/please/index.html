Please is a small word with a big effect, but it does not work the same way in every language. English speakers add please to nearly every request. In some languages the word exists but is used less often, because politeness is carried by the verb form or by tone instead. The table gives the usual word or phrase for please in each language.

Please inside the verb

Hindi speakers have kripya, but they more often show respect by using the polite verb ending: aap baithiye (please sit) is already polite without any extra word. The same is true in Punjabi, Marathi and Gujarati. Korean and Japanese build politeness into the whole sentence: the Japanese onegai shimasu works as "I request this", and the ending -te kudasai is the usual way to ask for an action. A request without the right ending can sound blunt however many pleases are added.

Please and "here you are"

German bitte, French s'il vous plaît (if it pleases you), Spanish por favor (as a favour), Italian per favore and Russian pozhaluysta are used for requests. German bitte also means you are welcome and "here you are", and Russian pozhaluysta also answers thank you. So one word covers three jobs, and a learner can get a long way with it.

Other ways to be polite

If you do not remember the word, polite phrasing works anywhere: ask with a question ("could you...?"), add the person's title, smile, and say thank you afterwards. In languages with a polite form of you (Hindi aap, Spanish usted, German Sie, French vous), choosing the polite form is itself a way of saying please.

Questions

Is please needed in Hindi?

It is not required in speech. The polite form "aap" and the verb ending carry respect. Add "kripya" in notices, letters and formal requests.

Does "bitte" also mean you are welcome?

Yes. In German it means please, you are welcome, here you are, and pardon, depending on the situation.
