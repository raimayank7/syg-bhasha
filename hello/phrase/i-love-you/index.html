I love you is among the most searched phrases of any language, and also the one most easily used wrongly. The table gives the sentence in each language, with a sound spelling. Read the notes below before using it, because a word that is natural in one language can be too strong, or too casual, in another.

The speaker's gender

In Hindi, Punjabi, Marathi and Urdu the verb changes with the gender of the speaker: a man says main tumse pyaar karta hoon and a woman says karti hoon. The table shows the masculine form; a woman should change the ending. In Arabic the word also changes with the person addressed: uhibbuka to a man and uhibbuki to a woman. Vietnamese has anh yêu em from a man to a younger woman and em yêu anh the other way around.

Strong and gentle words

Spanish has te quiero (I want you, used for family, friends and partners) and the stronger te amo (romantic love). Italian has ti voglio bene for friends and family and ti amo for a partner. Japanese suki desu (I like you) is the usual word, and ai shiteimasu is rare and very serious. In Chinese families the phrase wǒ ài nǐ is said less often than in English; affection is shown through food and care. In India, the sentence is seldom said aloud between parents and children, but it is common in films and between young couples.

A word of care

Use these words with people who would welcome them. In several of these societies a declaration of love to a person who does not want it can be taken as harassment, and sending it by message to someone you do not know is never acceptable. If you are learning the words for a partner, ask a native speaker how it is said between couples in their family.

Questions

Do women and men say I love you differently in Hindi?

Yes, the verb ending changes: "karta hoon" for a man and "karti hoon" for a woman.

Which language has the most tender word?

That is a matter of taste. Many people find the Persian-Urdu "mohabbat" and the Spanish "te amo" very romantic, but the best word is the one that is used in the family.
