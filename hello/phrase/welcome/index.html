Welcome is what a host says at the door, and what a shop or hotel puts on a sign. The table gives the common welcome in each language. In some languages the same word also answers thank you, as the notes explain.

Welcome to a guest

The Hindi swagat hai and Marathi swagat aahe use a Sanskrit word for a good arrival. The Arabic ahlan wa sahlan means "family and ease": you are among family and have ease. The Swahili karibu means "come near" and is also an answer to thank you and a welcome into a shop. The Turkish hoş geldiniz means "you came pleasantly" and is answered with hoş bulduk, "we found it pleasant". The Korean hwanyeonghamnida and the Japanese youkoso are the formal signs of welcome.

Welcome as a reply

In English, "you are welcome" answers thanks. Spanish de nada (it is nothing), French de rien and Portuguese de nada do the same job with different words. German bitte and Russian pozhaluysta serve as both please and you are welcome. In Hindi people reply koi baat nahin (no matter). These are separate phrases from the welcome at the door.

The guest in Indian homes

In Indian tradition the saying atithi devo bhava means "the guest is like God". A guest is greeted at the door, offered water and food, and told that the house is theirs. Many other cultures on this list, including the Arab, Turkish and Greek ones, have a similar custom, and the welcome word is the opening of it.

Questions

What do I say when someone says welcome?

Say thank you in their language. The table on the thank you page has the words.

Is "swagat" also used in English contexts?

Yes. Signs saying "Swagat" or "Welcome / स्वागत है" are common at stations, hotels and events across India.
