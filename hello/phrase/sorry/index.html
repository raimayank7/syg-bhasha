Sorry has two jobs: saying you are sorry for a mistake, and getting someone's attention or passing through a crowd (excuse me). Some languages use one phrase for both, others have two. The table gives a polite way to apologise in each language and the notes explain the difference.

Apology and attention

In Hindi, maaf kijiye means "please forgive" and is used for both an apology and "excuse me". Spanish has lo siento (I feel it) for a real apology and perdón or disculpe for excuse me. French has désolé for a true sorry and pardon or excusez-moi for attention. Japanese has gomen nasai for an apology and sumimasen for both "excuse me" and "thank you for the trouble". German Entschuldigung serves both purposes. Choose the phrase by what you mean.

Cultures of apology

In Japan and Korea an apology is a serious act, usually made with a bow, and the formal forms (moushiwake gozaimasen and joesonghamnida) carry real weight. In India, a short sorry in English is used everywhere, even inside sentences in other languages. In the Arab world, saying maalesh ("never mind") or ana aasif (I am sorry) with a hand on the chest is natural and brings warmth quickly. The Russian izvinite is polite for strangers; prosti is for friends.

If you have to apologise properly

A good apology says what happened, says you are sorry, and says what you will do. In any language, use the polite form and a calm tone, and do not add an excuse in the same breath. If you do not know the right words, a simple "sorry" and a small gesture will be understood.

Questions

What is the difference between sorry and excuse me?

Sorry is for a mistake you made. Excuse me is for getting attention or passing someone. Several languages use one phrase for both; the table shows the common phrase.

Is "maaf karna" correct in Punjabi?

Yes. "Maaf karna" is the usual form among friends and "maaf kar deo" is more respectful.
