Vietnamese phrases: hello, thank you, sorry and more

Common phrases and numbers in Vietnamese, with sound spelling for each.

Written by us from general knowledge. No native speaker has read this page yet, and usage varies by region. The sound spelling is approximate; capital letters mark the stressed syllable.

Vietnamese (Tiếng Việt) is a Austroasiatic language, written in the Latin with tone marks script. It is spoken in Vietnam, and Vietnamese communities in the United States, France, Australia and other countries. This page gives 12 everyday phrases and the numbers one to ten, each in its own writing with an easy sound spelling, followed by notes on how the words are used.

How the words are used

Vietnamese is tonal, with six tones marked by signs over or under the vowels. The sound spelling here is only a rough guide. The same syllable "ma" can mean ghost, mother, but, tomb, horse or rice seedling, depending on tone.

The writing uses the Latin alphabet with many marks, a system created in the 17th century and made official in the 20th. There are no word endings for tense or number, so word order and little helper words carry the meaning. How you address a person depends on age and relation: "anh" is an older brother or young man, "em" a younger sibling or young woman, and "chị" an older sister. That is why "I love you" comes out as "anh yêu em" (a man to a woman) or "em yêu anh" (a woman to a man).

The word "vâng" is a polite yes, mostly in the north, while "dạ" is used in the south. Friends and family say "ừ" or "có". For no, "không" is used. "Cảm ơn" is thank you, and adding "nhiều" (many) as in "cảm ơn nhiều" means thank you very much.

Questions

How do you say hello in Vietnamese?

Hello in Vietnamese is Xin chào, said "sin CHOW". See the notes on this page for when to use it.

How do you say thank you in Vietnamese?

Thank you in Vietnamese is Cảm ơn, said "kam UHN".

How do you count to ten in Vietnamese?

một (moht), hai (hai), ba (ba), bốn (bone), năm (nam), sáu (sow), bảy (bay), tám (tam), chín (cheen), mười (MUH-ee).
