German phrases: hello, thank you, sorry and more

Common phrases and numbers in German, with sound spelling for each.

Written by us from general knowledge. No native speaker has read this page yet, and usage varies by region. The sound spelling is approximate; capital letters mark the stressed syllable.

German (Deutsch) is a Germanic language, written in the Latin script. It is spoken in Germany, Austria, much of Switzerland, Liechtenstein, and parts of Italy, Belgium and Luxembourg. This page gives 12 everyday phrases and the numbers one to ten, each in its own writing with an easy sound spelling, followed by notes on how the words are used.

How the words are used

The German "w" sounds like an English "v", and "v" often sounds like "f", so "Wie" is "vee" and "vier" is "feer". The "ch" in "ich" and "dich" is a soft hiss made in the front of the mouth, close to the "h" in "huge"; in "acht" it is a rougher sound in the throat.

"Bitte" has many jobs: please, you are welcome, here you are, and pardon? Germans also use "Guten Tag" (good day) between about mid-morning and evening, and "Moin" in the north at any time. Younger people say "Tschüss" for goodbye among friends and "Auf Wiedersehen" in formal places.

German has two forms of you: "du" for friends and family and "Sie" for strangers, with a capital S. Nouns are always written with a capital letter. Learners often find the three genders of nouns (der, die, das) the hardest part of the language.

Questions

How do you say hello in German?

Hello in German is Hallo, said "HA-lo". See the notes on this page for when to use it.

How do you say thank you in German?

Thank you in German is Danke, said "DAN-ke".

How do you count to ten in German?

eins (ines), zwei (tsvy), drei (dry), vier (feer), fünf (fewnf), sechs (zeks), sieben (ZEE-ben), acht (akht), neun (noyn), zehn (tsayn).
