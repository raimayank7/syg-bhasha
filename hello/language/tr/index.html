Turkish phrases: hello, thank you, sorry and more

Common phrases and numbers in Turkish, with sound spelling for each.

Written by us from general knowledge. No native speaker has read this page yet, and usage varies by region. The sound spelling is approximate; capital letters mark the stressed syllable.

Turkish (Türkçe) is a Turkic language, written in the Latin script. It is spoken in Turkey and Northern Cyprus, and Turkish communities in Germany and other European countries. This page gives 12 everyday phrases and the numbers one to ten, each in its own writing with an easy sound spelling, followed by notes on how the words are used.

How the words are used

Turkish spelling is phonetic and uses a few special letters: "ç" is "ch", "ş" is "sh", "ğ" lengthens the vowel before it, and the dotless "ı" is a sound between "i" and "u" made with the lips flat. The dotted capital "İ" and dotless capital "I" are different letters.

Turkish builds words by adding endings in a fixed order, so one long word can be a whole sentence. "Teşekkür ederim" literally means "I make thanks", and its shorter form "teşekkürler" or the casual "sağ ol" are used with friends. Different goodbyes are used by the one who stays ("güle güle", go smiling) and the one who leaves ("hoşça kal", stay well).

The answer to "hoş geldiniz" (welcome) is "hoş bulduk" (we found it pleasant). Turkish has no gender in its words or its word for he or she ("o"), which makes it simple in that respect. Hospitality is central, and a guest is nearly always offered tea.

Questions

How do you say hello in Turkish?

Hello in Turkish is Merhaba, said "mer-ha-BA". See the notes on this page for when to use it.

How do you say thank you in Turkish?

Thank you in Turkish is Teşekkür ederim, said "te-shek-KEWR e-de-REEM".

How do you count to ten in Turkish?

bir (beer), iki (ee-KEE), üç (ewch), dört (durt), beş (besh), altı (al-TUH), yedi (ye-DEE), sekiz (se-KEEZ), dokuz (do-KOOZ), on (on).
