Swahili phrases: hello, thank you, sorry and more

Common phrases and numbers in Swahili, with sound spelling for each.

Written by us from general knowledge. No native speaker has read this page yet, and usage varies by region. The sound spelling is approximate; capital letters mark the stressed syllable.

Swahili (Kiswahili) is a Bantu language, written in the Latin script. It is spoken in Tanzania, Kenya, Uganda and the eastern region of the Democratic Republic of the Congo, as a common language across East Africa. This page gives 12 everyday phrases and the numbers one to ten, each in its own writing with an easy sound spelling, followed by notes on how the words are used.

How the words are used

Swahili is a Bantu language with many words borrowed from Arabic, which came through centuries of trade across the Indian Ocean. Some of them will be familiar: "kitabu" (book, from Arabic "kitab"), "safari" (journey, from Arabic) and "sabuni" (soap). A few come from Hindi and Persian too.

"Jambo" is the greeting that visitors learn first, and "hujambo" is the more correct form, with the reply "sijambo" (I have no problem). "Habari" means news, and the full question is "habari gani?" (what news?), answered with "nzuri" (good). "Karibu" means both come in and you are welcome, and it is the word heard in every shop and home.

Swahili spelling is regular, and the stress almost always falls on the second-last syllable. Words are built from a root with prefixes that mark the person, tense and object, so "nakupenda" is "na-" (I) + "ku-" (you) + "penda" (love), which gives I love you in one word.

Questions

How do you say hello in Swahili?

Hello in Swahili is Hujambo, said "hoo-JAM-bo". See the notes on this page for when to use it.

How do you say thank you in Swahili?

Thank you in Swahili is Asante, said "a-SAN-te".

How do you count to ten in Swahili?

moja (MO-ja), mbili (m-BEE-lee), tatu (TA-too), nne (N-ne), tano (TA-no), sita (SEE-ta), saba (SA-ba), nane (NA-ne), tisa (TEE-sa), kumi (KOO-mee).
