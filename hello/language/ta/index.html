Tamil phrases: hello, thank you, sorry and more

Common phrases and numbers in Tamil, with sound spelling for each.

Written by us from general knowledge. No native speaker has read this page yet, and usage varies by region. The sound spelling is approximate; capital letters mark the stressed syllable.

Tamil (தமிழ்) is a Dravidian language, written in the Tamil script. It is spoken in Tamil Nadu and Puducherry in India, northern Sri Lanka, Singapore, Malaysia and the Tamil diaspora around the world. This page gives 12 everyday phrases and the numbers one to ten, each in its own writing with an easy sound spelling, followed by notes on how the words are used.

How the words are used

"Vanakkam" is the all-day greeting, said with joined palms, and "nandri" means thank you. Tamil people leaving a house often say "poittu varen", which literally means "I will go and come", because saying "goodbye" outright can feel final. The same custom exists in several South Indian languages.

The letter written "zh" in the sound spelling is a special Tamil sound made with the tongue curled back; it is the sound in the name of the language itself, "Tamizh". English speakers may say it close to an "r" and still be understood.

Tamil is one of the oldest living classical languages. It has its own script with 12 vowels and 18 consonants as a core set, written from left to right. Tamil has no separate letters for aspirated sounds, so the same letter can sound like k or g, t or d, depending on its place in a word.

Questions

How do you say hello in Tamil?

Hello in Tamil is வணக்கம், said "va-NAK-kam". See the notes on this page for when to use it.

How do you say thank you in Tamil?

Thank you in Tamil is நன்றி, said "NAN-dri".

How do you count to ten in Tamil?

ஒன்று (ON-dru), இரண்டு (i-RAN-du), மூன்று (MOON-dru), நான்கு (NAAN-gu), ஐந்து (ain-DU), ஆறு (AA-ru), ஏழு (E-zhu), எட்டு (ET-tu), ஒன்பது (on-ba-DU), பத்து (pat-TU).
