Telugu phrases: hello, thank you, sorry and more

Common phrases and numbers in Telugu, with sound spelling for each.

Written by us from general knowledge. No native speaker has read this page yet, and usage varies by region. The sound spelling is approximate; capital letters mark the stressed syllable.

Telugu (తెలుగు) is a Dravidian language, written in the Telugu script. It is spoken in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana in India, and Telugu communities in the United States and the Gulf. This page gives 12 everyday phrases and the numbers one to ten, each in its own writing with an easy sound spelling, followed by notes on how the words are used.

How the words are used

Telugu is often called the Italian of the East because its words end in vowels, which makes it sound smooth. "Namaskaram" works at any time of day, and the respectful "meeru" is the usual word for you with elders and strangers.

Like Tamil, Telugu speakers say "velloostanu" (I will go) when they leave, rather than a hard goodbye. The word for yes, "avunu", and the word for no, "kaadu", are sometimes shortened in speech to "aa" and "kaadu".

The script is round, which is often explained by the old palm-leaf writing, where straight strokes could split the leaf. It is written from left to right. Telugu shares many letters with Kannada, and a reader of one can learn the other quickly.

Questions

How do you say hello in Telugu?

Hello in Telugu is నమస్కారం, said "na-mas-KAA-ram". See the notes on this page for when to use it.

How do you say thank you in Telugu?

Thank you in Telugu is ధన్యవాదాలు, said "dhan-ya-VAA-daa-lu".

How do you count to ten in Telugu?

ఒకటి (o-KA-ti), రెండు (REN-du), మూడు (MOO-du), నాలుగు (NAA-lu-gu), ఐదు (AI-du), ఆరు (AA-ru), ఏడు (E-du), ఎనిమిది (e-ni-MI-di), తొమ్మిది (tom-MI-di), పది (pa-DI).
