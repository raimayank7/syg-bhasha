Portuguese phrases: hello, thank you, sorry and more

Common phrases and numbers in Portuguese, with sound spelling for each.

Written by us from general knowledge. No native speaker has read this page yet, and usage varies by region. The sound spelling is approximate; capital letters mark the stressed syllable.

Portuguese (Português) is a Romance language, written in the Latin script. It is spoken in Portugal, Brazil, Angola, Mozambique, and other countries and regions, including Goa in India. This page gives 12 everyday phrases and the numbers one to ten, each in its own writing with an easy sound spelling, followed by notes on how the words are used.

How the words are used

A man says "obrigado" for thank you and a woman says "obrigada", because the word means obliged and agrees with the speaker. The same is true of "bem-vindo" and "bem-vinda" for welcome. Brazilians commonly use "valeu" among friends as an informal thanks.

Portuguese has nasal vowels, marked by the tilde (ã, õ) or by "m" and "n" at the end of a syllable, so "não" ends with a soft nasal sound close to "wng". Brazilian and European Portuguese sound quite different: Brazilians say "tchau" and the "d" in "dia" like "j" in "jeep", while in Portugal the unstressed vowels are swallowed.

For "you", Brazil uses "você" for most people and "tu" in some regions, while Portugal uses "tu" for friends and "o senhor" or "a senhora" for elders. The word "saudade", a deep longing for someone or something absent, is often called one of the hardest words to translate.

Questions

How do you say hello in Portuguese?

Hello in Portuguese is Olá, said "o-LA". See the notes on this page for when to use it.

How do you say thank you in Portuguese?

Thank you in Portuguese is Obrigado, said "o-bri-GA-do".

How do you count to ten in Portuguese?

um (oom), dois (doys), três (trays), quatro (KWA-tro), cinco (SEEN-ko), seis (says), sete (SE-te), oito (OY-to), nove (NO-ve), dez (dess).
