Korean phrases: hello, thank you, sorry and more

Common phrases and numbers in Korean, with sound spelling for each.

Written by us from general knowledge. No native speaker has read this page yet, and usage varies by region. The sound spelling is approximate; capital letters mark the stressed syllable.

Korean (한국어) is a Koreanic language, written in the Hangul script. It is spoken in South Korea and North Korea, and Korean communities in China, Japan and the United States. This page gives 12 everyday phrases and the numbers one to ten, each in its own writing with an easy sound spelling, followed by notes on how the words are used.

How the words are used

Korean has two goodbyes. You say "annyeonghi gaseyo" (go in peace) to the person who is leaving and "annyeonghi gyeseyo" (stay in peace) when you are the one leaving. Both begin with "annyeong", which also means hello, and the casual "annyeong" alone is used between friends and close family.

Hangul, the Korean alphabet, was designed in the 15th century and is one of the most logical writing systems in the world. Its letters are grouped in square blocks of one syllable each, and a learner can read it aloud after a day or two of study. The numbers here are the Sino-Korean ones used for dates, money and phone numbers; Korean also has a second set (hana, dul, set) for counting objects and age.

Speech levels matter. The "-yo" ending is polite and everyday, the "-mnida" ending is formal, and plain endings are for close friends. Bowing a little while greeting is the normal custom.

Questions

How do you say hello in Korean?

Hello in Korean is 안녕하세요, said "an-nyeong-ha-SE-yo". See the notes on this page for when to use it.

How do you say thank you in Korean?

Thank you in Korean is 감사합니다, said "gam-sa-ham-NI-da".

How do you count to ten in Korean?

일 (il), 이 (ee), 삼 (sam), 사 (sa), 오 (oh), 육 (yuk), 칠 (chil), 팔 (pal), 구 (gu), 십 (ship).
