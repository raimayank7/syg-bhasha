Malayalam phrases: hello, thank you, sorry and more

Common phrases and numbers in Malayalam, with sound spelling for each.

Written by us from general knowledge. No native speaker has read this page yet, and usage varies by region. The sound spelling is approximate; capital letters mark the stressed syllable.

Malayalam (മലയാളം) is a Dravidian language, written in the Malayalam script. It is spoken in Kerala, Lakshadweep and Mahe in India, and a large community in the Gulf countries. This page gives 12 everyday phrases and the numbers one to ten, each in its own writing with an easy sound spelling, followed by notes on how the words are used.

How the words are used

"Namaskaram" is the usual greeting. A very common everyday question is "sukhamano?", which means "is everything well?", and the answer is "sukham". Malayalis who work abroad often greet one another in English or Arabic phrases too, so you will hear a mix.

Like Tamil, Malayalam has the special sound written "zh" (as in "ezhu", seven). It is a retroflex sound made with the tongue curled back. English speakers often say it close to an "r" or a soft "l" and are understood.

The script is round and full of loops, written from left to right. It has more letters than most other Indian scripts, because it keeps the sounds of Sanskrit as well as those of Malayalam itself. The word for goodbye, "poyi varam" (I will go and return), follows the South Indian custom of avoiding a final goodbye.

Questions

How do you say hello in Malayalam?

Hello in Malayalam is നമസ്കാരം, said "na-mas-KAA-ram". See the notes on this page for when to use it.

How do you say thank you in Malayalam?

Thank you in Malayalam is നന്ദി, said "NAN-di".

How do you count to ten in Malayalam?

ഒന്ന് (ON-nu), രണ്ട് (RAN-du), മൂന്ന് (MOON-nu), നാല് (NAA-lu), അഞ്ച് (AN-chu), ആറ് (AA-ru), ഏഴ് (E-zhu), എട്ട് (ET-tu), ഒമ്പത് (OM-pa-thu), പത്ത് (PAT-thu).
