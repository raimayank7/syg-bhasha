French phrases: hello, thank you, sorry and more

Common phrases and numbers in French, with sound spelling for each.

Written by us from general knowledge. No native speaker has read this page yet, and usage varies by region. The sound spelling is approximate; capital letters mark the stressed syllable.

French (Français) is a Romance language, written in the Latin script. It is spoken in France, Belgium, Switzerland, Canada, and many countries of West and Central Africa. This page gives 12 everyday phrases and the numbers one to ten, each in its own writing with an easy sound spelling, followed by notes on how the words are used.

How the words are used

French has one word, "bonjour", for hello and good morning, and it is used until the evening, when people switch to "bonsoir". In shops it is rude to start talking without saying bonjour first. Friends say "salut" for hello and goodbye.

Spelling is not close to sound, and many final letters are silent: "merci" ends in a sounded "i", but "vous" ends in nothing. Words flow into each other, so "s'il vous plaît" sounds like one long word. Use the sound spelling as a guide and listen to a speaker.

Like Spanish, French has two forms of you: "tu" for friends and "vous" for strangers, elders and groups. To a stranger always use "vous" and "monsieur" or "madame". "Je t'aime" is a serious statement of love, kept for partners and sometimes close family.

Questions

How do you say hello in French?

Hello in French is Bonjour, said "bon-ZHOOR". See the notes on this page for when to use it.

How do you say thank you in French?

Thank you in French is Merci, said "mer-SEE".

How do you count to ten in French?

un (uhn), deux (duh), trois (trwa), quatre (KA-truh), cinq (sank), six (sees), sept (set), huit (weet), neuf (nuhf), dix (dees).
